## Verified Phase 1 Findings

- The India Code consolidated SC/ST Act PDF is marked “As on the 21st September, 2025” and lists the 2015 Amendment Act, 2018 Amendment Act and Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 as amending enactments.
- The SC/ST Act has been extracted section-by-section from sections 1-23 and the Schedule, with operative subject and interaction points recorded in `legislation/SCST_ACT_CLAUSE_EXTRACTION.md`.
- Section 18A was inserted by the 2018 Amendment Act and is shown as effective from 20-08-2018.
- The J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 removed the former J&K exclusion from section 1(2), effective 31-10-2019.
- India Code's Act metadata still reports 19-11-2018 as last updated. This is retained as metadata, not treated as a completeness cutoff.
- The identified SC/ST Rules amendment chain through 2018 is verified at instrument level: 1995 principal Rules, G.S.R. 896(E) 2011, G.S.R. 725(E) 2013, G.S.R. 416(E) 2014, G.S.R. 774(E) 2014, G.S.R. 424(E) 2016 and G.S.R. 588(E) 2018.
- Ministry of Law and Justice notification S.O. 2790(E), dated 16-07-2024, is a primary transition instrument for references to IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act provisions in Acts, rules, notifications and related instruments, directing corresponding BNS, BNSS and BSA references.
- BNS section 358 repeals IPC with savings. BNSS section 531 repeals CrPC with savings. BSA section 170 repeals the Indian Evidence Act with savings. These transition provisions are relevant to the SC/ST Act's legacy references.
- The consolidated SC/ST Rules extraction records Rules 1-18, the 47-item Schedule relief table, Annexure-I and Annexure-II/attached disability material.
- Rule 2(ga) expressly carries the IPC section 39 definition of “voluntarily”, creating a BNS transition item.
- Rule 7 contains a 60-day investigation/charge-sheet framework and requires a controlled BNSS interface analysis.
- Rule 12 contains the seven-day relief framework and preserves other-law compensation rights.
- Rule 16's former 25-member ceiling was removed/omitted in the 2018 amendment.
- Schedule items 24-32, 40-41 and 44 contain IPC terminology and require conduct-level BNS mapping.
- Schedule item 43 presents a material source discrepancy: the consolidated table cites a 01-06-2001 disability notification while the attached Annexure-II transcription begins with a 06-08-1986 notification. This remains unresolved and prevents a full primary-source freeze of Annexure-II.
- The Department of Social Justice and Empowerment current publication index, last updated 22-06-2026, lists the 1995 Rules and separate 2016 and 2018 PoA Rules publications. This is a current-source indicator, not proof that no later instrument exists.
- The section 3 BNS crosswalk maps every section 3(1) conduct at the level of exact, partial or absent BNS correspondence and maps all 32 distinct IPC references in the Act Schedule under section 3(2)(va).
- Material BNS differences identified include consolidation of IPC clusters into BNS sections 61, 115-118, 124, 126, 137, 140, 189, 191 and 351; the BNS grievous-hurt 15-day threshold; increased punishment ceiling for armed rioting; changed fines; and restructuring of IPC 376C into BNS 68.
- Rule 2(ga)'s IPC section 39 reference corresponds to BNS section 2(33), with the core intention/knowledge test retained.
- BNSS section 4(2) makes BNSS procedure applicable to offences under other laws subject to special enactments regulating manner/place; section 5 preserves special/local laws and special jurisdiction/procedure.
- BNSS section 531 repeals the CrPC and preserves specified pending appeals, applications, trials, inquiries and investigations under the CrPC as at 01-07-2024, while deeming specified pre-existing procedural instruments to correspond to BNSS provisions.
- The BNSS procedural crosswalk maps the defined Act/Rules interfaces covering special courts/direct cognizance, investigation/FIR, Rule 7 DSP-level investigation and 60-day charge-sheet framework, arrest, bail/anticipatory bail, appeals, externment, preventive action, victim/witness rights, property attachment/forfeiture, probation and override.
- BSA Act 47/2023 came into force on 01-07-2024 and applies to judicial proceedings in or before a Court subject to s.1(2).
- BSA s.170(1) repeals the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, while s.170(2) preserves the former Act for applications, trials, inquiries, investigations, proceedings and appeals pending immediately before BSA commencement.
- BSA s.2 defines “document” to include electronic and digital records and “evidence” to include electronically given statements and electronic/digital records produced for Court inspection; s.2(l) defines “shall presume”.
- BSA ss.61-63 provide the current electronic-record admissibility/proof framework, including the statutory conditions and certificate requirement under s.63(4).
- BSA ss.104-109 provide general burden-of-proof rules. BSA s.115 is a distinct presumption concerning specified offences/areas and is not an equivalent of Act s.8.
- Act s.8(a)-(b) contains two special presumptions for SC/ST Act prosecutions. No BSA provision identified in the Phase 1 crosswalk duplicates those special presumptions.
- Evidence-related aspects of Act s.3(2)(i)-(ii) and (vi), including false/fabricated evidence, disappearance of evidence and false information, operate within the BSA's general relevance, proof, burden and witness framework; the BSA does not replace the substantive SC/ST Act offences.
- Rules 5-7, 12 and materially relevant implementation/reporting provisions create records that may become documentary/electronic evidence, but the Rules do not create a general special evidentiary presumption for those records.

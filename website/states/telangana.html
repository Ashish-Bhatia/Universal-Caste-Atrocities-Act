Telangana

Telangana

Phase 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map

Scope

This jurisdiction inventory covers Telangana-specific implementation material for the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and Rules, related Protection of Civil Rights interfaces, State institutional arrangements, police machinery, courts/prosecutors, relief, monitoring committees and recent official corroboration. It is an existing-law inventory, not a policy-superiority or constitutional-validity conclusion.

1. Governing instruments and State administrative layer

Telangana's Scheduled Castes Development Department is the principal State administrative layer identified in the located official material for implementation of the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 and SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989. A current Nizamabad district government page states that the District Scheduled Caste Development Department is responsible for implementation of both Acts and for monitoring related schemes. [A: TG-STATE-001]

Official Telangana district government GO repositories identify State orders concerning relief and rehabilitation under the PoA Act and later enhancement of compensation, including G.O.Ms.No.6, G.O.Ms.No.13 and G.O.Ms.No.29 listed by the Khammam district administration. The underlying orders should be retrieved directly during the later instrument census. [A/B: TG-STATE-002]

The State budget and planning material also carries a specific scheme for monetary relief and legal aid to victims of atrocities and a separate budget head for special criminal courts dealing with offences against SC/ST persons. [A: TG-STATE-003, TG-STATE-004]

2. Rule 9 nodal architecture

The Ministry's State-specific implementation report for Telangana records that the Commissioner, Scheduled Castes Development Department was designated as the Rule 9 Nodal Officer for coordinating District Magistrates and Superintendents of Police. [A: TG-STATE-005]

The current 2025-26 Telangana Government employee-strength publication continues to identify the Scheduled Castes Development Department as an active Secretariat and field department, but it does not by itself establish the current named Rule 9 appointment. [A: TG-STATE-006]

Current named-office and successor-order verification remains open.

3. Rule 8 Protection Cell and police machinery

The Ministry's Telangana implementation report for 2020 records a State-level SC/ST Protection Cell headed by an Inspector General of Police (CID), functioning under the control and supervision of the Additional Director General of Police (CID). It records 30 police units, comprising 9 Commissionerates, 20 Police Districts and 1 Railway Police District, through which atrocity cases were registered. It also records eight regional CID offices and DSP-level enquiries referred by the ADGP, CID, including the PCR Cell at CID, Hyderabad. [A: TG-STATE-005]

A Telangana Scheduled Castes Development Department handbook describes a PCR Cell in CBCID as an institutional unit dealing with SC/ST Atrocities and PCR Act cases and associates Rule 8 with the State Protection Cell. [B: TG-STATE-007]

The located material establishes a Protection Cell/PCR-CID architecture for the relevant reporting periods. It does not establish a complete 2026 Rule 8 staffing chart or current reporting matrix. A current dedicated Special Police Station census was not independently established. Search silence is not treated as proof of absence. [A/B: TG-STATE-005, TG-STATE-007]

4. Rule 7 investigation and charge-sheet timing

The Ministry's Telangana implementation report for 2020 records 302 cases in which investigation and filing of charge-sheets were completed within 60 days and 507 cases in which filing occurred later than 60 days. [A: TG-STATE-005]

The Ministry's 2022 Section 21(4) reporting data, as reproduced in a 2026 secondary analysis citing the Ministry report, records 823 charge-sheets within 60 days and 1268 after 60 days for Telangana. This secondary reproduction is retained as corroboration rather than a substitute for direct file verification. [C: TG-STATE-008]

A 2024 Rachakonda Police Commissionerate annual report records a 2024 case registered under BNS section 70(1) read with section 3(2)(v) of the SC/ST PoA Act at Vanasthalipuram Police Station. This provides current police corroboration that Telangana police units were registering and investigating PoA-linked offences after the new criminal codes came into force. [A: TG-STATE-009]

A current statewide post-BNS/BNSS/BSA instruction set, Rule 7 compliance series and written reasons for delayed investigation remain open.

5. Rule 10 / identified and atrocity-prone areas

The Ministry's Telangana implementation report records Joint Collectors as Special Officers in districts having atrocity-prone areas. [A: TG-STATE-005]

The Ministry's official 2022 identified-area answer records 66 villages as atrocity-prone/identified areas in Telangana: Nizamabad Commissionerate 18; Rachakonda Commissionerate 5; Ramagundam Commissionerate 9; Adilabad 4; Bhadradri-Kothagudem 17; Jagityal 1; Mahabubnagar 5; Nalgonda 6; and Narayanpet 1. [A: TG-STATE-010]

The 66-village list is a reporting snapshot. Current 2026 identified-area orders, successor district nomenclature, current village list and instrument basis remain unverified.

6. Vigilance and Monitoring Committees

The Ministry's Telangana implementation report for 2020 records a State Level Vigilance and Monitoring Committee meeting on 05-11-2020, District Level V&MCs functioning in all districts with 32 meetings across 29 districts, and Sub-Divisional V&MCs constituted in 12 districts with 13 meetings across eight districts. [A: TG-STATE-005]

For calendar year 2022, the Ministry's Section 21(4) data as reproduced in a 2026 analysis records one State-level V&MC meeting and 67 District-level meetings against 132 mandatory quarterly district meetings. The source is a secondary reproduction of the Ministry's 2022 report and is therefore retained at C grade pending direct file verification. [C: TG-STATE-008]

The located sources do not establish a complete current 2026 State/District/Sub-Divisional V&MC calendar, minutes, membership or compliance matrix.

7. Special and Exclusive Special Courts

A Telangana Scheduled Castes Development Department handbook records ten Special Sessions Courts established for SC/ST atrocity cases and Additional Public Prosecutors (Grade-I) appointed for those courts. The handbook also states that nine Mobile Courts were sanctioned for PCR Act matters. [B: TG-STATE-007]

The Ministry's 2022 implementation data, as reproduced by a 2026 secondary analysis, identifies 10 Exclusive Special Court districts in Telangana out of 33 districts. This is retained as secondary corroboration because the direct 2022 Ministry PDF was not independently fetched in this session. [C: TG-STATE-008]

A Telangana High Court decision from 2022 refers to proceedings before a Special Judge and addresses the jurisdiction of the Special Court in relation to other offences. It is retained as institutional/jurisdictional corroboration only and not as Phase 2 merits research. [A/C: TG-STATE-011]

Current district-by-district Special/Exclusive Special Court notifications and post-reorganisation court census remain open.

8. SPP/ESPP

The Telangana departmental handbook states that Additional Public Prosecutors (Grade-I) were appointed for the ten Special Sessions Courts. [B: TG-STATE-007]

The 2019 UPR stakeholders report, using the Ministry's 2017 Section 21(4) data, records ten Telangana Special Courts and ten Special Public Prosecutors for that historical reporting period. [B/C: TG-STATE-012]

The current 2026 named SPP/ESPP roster, tenure, court allocation and whether each prosecutor is exclusive to PoA matters remain unverified.

9. Relief, rehabilitation and victim/witness expenses

Telangana maintains a specific State scheme for monetary relief and legal aid to victims of atrocities. Telangana budget documents identify the scheme as a State expenditure item, and the 2024 State Special Development Fund document records a combined centrally sponsored/state-share provision of Rs. 2,445.48 lakh for monetary relief and legal aid to victims of atrocities on SCs. [A: TG-STATE-003, TG-STATE-004]

The current Warangal district government page states that victims of atrocities on SCs/STs receive monetary relief and legal aid through the District Collector. The page was last updated 02-09-2026 and therefore provides current administrative corroboration of the relief/legal-aid delivery layer, without establishing case-level statutory timing compliance. [A: TG-STATE-013]

The Ministry's current centrally reported scheme table records 5,287 persons receiving relief in Telangana in 2020-21, 2,284 in 2021-22, 2,500 in 2022-23, 2,332 in 2023-24 and 3,635 in 2024-25. These are scheme reporting figures and are not treated as proof of seven-day statutory compliance. [A: TG-STATE-014]

Current relief rates, sanction/payment workflow, seven-day compliance, travel/maintenance/diet allowance workflow and victim/witness support remain open.

10. State PoA/PCR instruments and related-law interfaces

Official Telangana district GO repositories identify State orders for relief/rehabilitation and enhancement of compensation under the PoA Act, including 2018 amendment-rule material and later compensation orders. Direct instrument retrieval and validity/currentness checking remain pending. [A/B: TG-STATE-002]

The Telangana Scheduled Castes Development Department handbook describes the PCR Cell/CBCID architecture, special courts and police training on the 2015/2016 PoA amendments. It also records a scheme for monetary relief and legal aid. [B: TG-STATE-007]

The 2024 Rachakonda Police annual report demonstrates practical interface with BNS and the PoA Act in a recorded 2024 case. It is not treated as evidence of a complete State-wide transition protocol. [A: TG-STATE-009]

11. Annual reporting and statistics

The 2020 Ministry implementation report is the most directly retrievable detailed Telangana institutional snapshot located in this session. It records one State V&MC meeting, 32 District V&MC meetings in 29 districts, 13 Sub-Divisional meetings in eight districts, a State Protection Cell under IG CID/ADGP CID supervision, 30 police units, eight regional CID offices, 302 timely charge-sheets and 507 delayed charge-sheets. [A: TG-STATE-005]

The Ministry's 2022 implementation data, as reproduced in a 2026 secondary analysis, records one State V&MC meeting, 67 District V&MC meetings, 823 charge-sheets within 60 days, 1268 after 60 days and 18 persons receiving legal aid. These figures require direct Ministry file verification before use as final primary-source statistics. [C: TG-STATE-008]

The Ministry's 2020 crime table records 1,842 SC cases and 545 ST cases under the PoA Act in Telangana, total 2,387. [A: TG-STATE-015]

The Ministry's current centrally reported relief table records 3,635 persons receiving relief in Telangana in 2024-25. [A: TG-STATE-014]

12. Legal-aid and victim-support interface

The State budget explicitly provides for monetary relief and legal aid to victims of atrocities, and the current Warangal district government page states that the District Collector is the channel through which monetary relief and legal aid are provided. [A: TG-STATE-003, TG-STATE-013]

The 2020 Ministry report and departmental handbook establish the historical institutional architecture. A complete 2026 victim/witness referral, legal-aid, protection, travel and maintenance workflow was not independently verified.

13. Current/recent judicial and police corroboration

The 2024 Rachakonda Police Commissionerate annual report records a case under BNS section 70(1) read with section 3(2)(v) of the SC/ST PoA Act, 1989 at Vanasthalipuram Police Station. This is direct State police evidence of post-BNS operational interaction with the PoA Act. [A: TG-STATE-009]

The current Hyderabad district Collectorate page, last updated 03-09-2026, lists atrocities against SCs/STs and the PoA/PCR Act within its law-and-order functions. This confirms continuing district-administration handling of PoA/PCR matters but does not establish a complete State-wide workflow. [A: TG-STATE-016]

The 2022 Telangana High Court decision involving a Special Judge is retained as historical institutional corroboration only. [A: TG-STATE-011]

14. Currentness limitations

The following remain unverified or incompletely verified as of 2026-09-05:

  • current named Rule 9 Nodal Officer and successor appointment instrument;
  • current Rule 8 Protection Cell staffing and reporting matrix;
  • current dedicated Special Police Station status, including whether any unit is separately notified;
  • current Rule 10 Special Officer orders and current identified-area notifications;
  • current district-by-district identified/atrocity-prone village list;
  • current State/District/Sub-Divisional V&MC membership, meeting calendars and minutes;
  • current district-by-district Special/Exclusive Special Court notifications and census;
  • current named SPP/ESPP roster, tenure and allocation;
  • current relief rates, sanction/payment workflow and seven-day compliance;
  • current victim/witness travel, maintenance and dietary allowance workflow;
  • later Section 21(4) reports after 2022 as directly verified primary material;
  • direct file-level retrieval of every Telangana GO cited in district repositories;
  • current post-BNS/BNSS/BSA police/prosecution transition instructions;
  • current PoA-specific legal-aid and victim/witness referral workflow;
  • reconciliation of Telangana's pre- and post-2016 district/commissionerate structures with current 2026 administrative geography.

Chandigarh

Chandigarh

Phase 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map

Scope

This jurisdiction inventory records Chandigarh-specific implementation material for the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and Rules, including administrative machinery, courts/prosecutors, relief, monitoring and current/recent corroboration. It is an existing-law inventory. It does not assess policy superiority, necessity or constitutional validity.

1. Beneficiary scope and territorial context

Chandigarh has a notified Scheduled Caste population. The Census of India 2011 maintains Chandigarh-specific Scheduled Caste datasets and lists multiple notified Scheduled Caste communities for the UT. [A: CH-STATE-001]

Official Ministry of Tribal Affairs material states that no community has been specified as a Scheduled Tribe in relation to Chandigarh. [A: CH-STATE-002]

Accordingly, the Chandigarh implementation baseline is principally an SC implementation baseline. The absence of a notified ST community in Chandigarh does not support any broader conclusion about future statutory scope or non-statutory tribal identity.

2. Rule 9 Nodal Officer

The Chandigarh Administration's 16 October 2015 Gazette notification, expressly superseding the Personnel Department order of 24 June 2002, appoints the Secretary, Social Welfare, Chandigarh Administration as Nodal Officer under Rule 9. The notification assigns coordination with the District Magistrate, Superintendent of Police, investigating officers and other implementing officers. [A: CH-STATE-003]

The 2022 Section 21(4) report repeats the Secretary, Social Welfare Department as the Rule 9 Nodal Officer. [A: CH-STATE-004]

Current 2026 holder by name/office posting was not independently verified. The operative instrument itself is verified, but current incumbent verification remains open.

3. Rule 8 Protection Cell and investigation machinery

A Chandigarh Administration Home Department order dated 17 April 2015 constituted the SC/ST Protection Cell under Rule 8(i). The order identifies the Inspector General of Police, UT Chandigarh as Chairman, with the Sub-Divisional Magistrate (Central), Director Social Welfare and Deputy Superintendent of Police (W&CSU) as members. It assigns functions including survey of identified areas, public-order measures, recommendations for special police force/posts, investigation into probable causes, restoration of security, review of investigations and spot inspections, enquiries into refusal to record information or willful negligence, review of registered cases, and monthly reporting to the Nodal Officer. [A: CH-STATE-005]

The 2021 Chandigarh Social Audit/RTI compilation records the same Protection Cell order and describes the cell as under the Chairmanship of the Director General of Police, UT Chandigarh. [B: CH-STATE-006]

The 2022 Ministry report states that the Protection Cell is under the supervision of the Director General of Police, Chandigarh. [A: CH-STATE-004]

These sources establish the formal Rule 8 machinery and its historical/current-reporting-period supervision, but current 2026 staffing and deployment remain open.

4. Special Police Stations and investigation

The 2021 Chandigarh Social Audit/RTI compilation expressly records that no Special Police Station for SC/ST offences had been set up and that complaints/cases were dealt with in the concerned police stations. [B: CH-STATE-006]

No later Chandigarh Administration notification establishing dedicated Special Police Stations was independently retrieved. Search silence is not treated as proof of present absence.

The central Rules require investigation by a police officer not below Deputy Superintendent of Police rank. [A: CH-STATE-007]

A current Chandigarh-specific officer roster for PoA investigations was not independently verified. This remains open.

5. Rule 10 / atrocity-prone areas

The Chandigarh Administration's 2015 Special Officer order appoints the Director Social Welfare as Special Officer under Rule 10 for coordination with the District Magistrate, Superintendent of Police, implementing officers, committees and the Protection Cell. [A: CH-STATE-008]

Ministry Section 21(4) reporting for 2019 and 2022 states that there was no atrocity-prone area in Chandigarh, while retaining the Director Social Welfare as Special Officer. [A: CH-STATE-004, CH-STATE-009]

This is a reporting-period finding. A current 2026 Rule 3/Rule 10 review, notification corpus and present-area determination remain open.

6. Vigilance and Monitoring Committees

The Chandigarh Administration records a State Level Vigilance and Monitoring Committee constituted under Notification No. SW1/Atro/2004/912 dated 17 March 2004, with a later reconstitution notification dated 5 December 2016. [A: CH-STATE-010]

The 2021 Chandigarh Social Audit/RTI compilation records that Chandigarh is a single-district city-state and that the State Level V&MC was constituted to review implementation, relief and rehabilitation and related matters. [B: CH-STATE-006]

Ministry reporting records one State-level V&MC meeting on 11 February 2019 and no meeting during 2022. [A: CH-STATE-009, CH-STATE-004]

The 2022 report does not establish current 2026 meeting compliance. Because Chandigarh is a single-district UT, the relationship between the UT/state-level committee and any district/sub-divisional committee machinery requires current instrument-level verification rather than inference from the single-district structure.

7. Special Courts and Exclusive Special Courts

A Chandigarh Administration Home Department notification dated 2 November 2016, published through the Administration's official instruments, superseded the 2014 designation and specified the Court of First Additional Sessions Judge, UT Chandigarh as a Special Court under Section 14(1). [A: CH-STATE-011]

A later judicial-administration notification dated 6 March 2024 superseded the 2016 notification and specified the Courts of Sessions Judge and Additional Sessions Judge-II, III, IV and V, in addition to Additional Sessions Judge-I, as Special Courts under Section 14(1), within their respective jurisdictions. [A: CH-STATE-012]

The 2022 Ministry report predates the 2024 redesignation and therefore cannot be used as the current court designation. The 2024 instrument is the controlling current designation located in this pass.

No separate Exclusive Special Court designation was independently identified. The 2021 Chandigarh Social Audit/RTI compilation states that no Exclusive Special Court had been set up because of the negligible reported case burden at that time. [B: CH-STATE-006]

8. SPP / ESPP

The Chandigarh Administration's 16 October 2015 notification records that the Deputy District Attorneys/Additional Public Prosecutors of the Law & Prosecution Department deputed/posted in the Special Court are specified as Special Public Prosecutors for conducting cases. [A: CH-STATE-013]

The 2021 Chandigarh Social Audit/RTI compilation repeats this arrangement and states that no Exclusive Special Public Prosecutor had been appointed. [B: CH-STATE-006]

The 2022 Ministry report likewise states that Deputy District Attorneys/Additional Public Prosecutors, Chandigarh, were specified as Special Public Prosecutors. [A: CH-STATE-004]

A current 2026 named SPP/ESPP roster, tenure, three-year notification cycle and court allocation were not independently verified. This remains open.

9. Relief, legal aid and rehabilitation

The 2022 Ministry report records that four victims of atrocities were provided relief during 2022. It also records that Chandigarh had formulated and notified rules for legal aid to Scheduled Castes and that free legal aid was being provided by the State Legal Services Authority, UT Chandigarh. [A: CH-STATE-004]

The Ministry's current CSS data records central assistance released to Chandigarh of Rs. 0.50 crore in 2020-21, Rs. 0.71 crore in 2021-22, Rs. 1.71 crore in 2022-23, Rs. 1.73 crore in 2023-24 and Rs. 1.62 crore in 2024-25. The same official dataset records zero persons provided relief under the scheme in each of those five years, while separately recording inter-caste marriage incentive beneficiaries. [A: CH-STATE-014]

These datasets concern different reporting concepts and must not be treated as contradictory without reconciliation. Central assistance released is not equivalent to case-level relief paid to victims. The 2022 annual report's four victims receiving relief is case-level reporting for that year and does not establish the five-year central dataset's relief-recipient measure.

Current statutory relief rates, payment workflow, seven-day compliance, victim/witness travel and maintenance, case-level sanction records and payment auditability remain open.

10. Annual reporting and statistical context

The Ministry's annual-report archive identifies the 2022 Section 21(4) report as the recent Chandigarh-specific institutional snapshot located for this inventory, with earlier 2021 reporting also available. [A: CH-STATE-004, CH-STATE-015]

The 2021 Ministry case table records Chandigarh with zero reported cases in its Chandigarh row for the relevant table. [A: CH-STATE-016]

The 2022 Ministry case table records Chandigarh with three cases in the reporting table. The table must be read using its own column headings and reporting definitions. [A: CH-STATE-017]

These figures are historical reporting-period data, not a 2026 crime-rate conclusion. The Ministry archive also confirms continued publication of PoA reports and current scheme materials. [A: CH-STATE-015]

11. Current/recent judicial and police corroboration

The Punjab and Haryana High Court's 2024 decision in Dinesh Jangra v. State of U.T. Chandigarh and another involved allegations under the SC/ST PoA Act and addressed the prima facie applicability of the Act in the context of anticipatory bail. [A: CH-STATE-018]

The High Court's official case materials also show continuing Section 14A SC/ST bail/appellate work in its Chandigarh jurisdiction. [A: CH-STATE-019]

These sources are used only as institutional/current judicial corroboration. Phase 2 merits synthesis has not been undertaken.

12. Related-law interfaces

The Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 is administered alongside the PoA Act through the Chandigarh Social Welfare machinery. India Code records both statutes in Chandigarh's Social Welfare and Police Department collections. [A: CH-STATE-020]

The 2024 judicial-administration materials and Chandigarh administrative records also show overlap between PoA Special Courts and the broader Sessions judiciary. This is an administrative interface, not evidence of legal duplication or conflict.

BNS/BNSS/BSA transition verification remains a separate national workstream. The 2024 Dinesh Jangra decision is not used to infer a complete transition position.

13. Currentness limitations

The following remain unverified or incompletely verified as of 2026-09-05:

  • current incumbent of the Rule 9 Nodal Officer office;
  • current Rule 8 Protection Cell staffing, deployment and reporting workload;
  • current investigating-officer roster and supervision arrangements;
  • current dedicated Special Police Station status;
  • current Rule 3/Rule 10 Special Officer and atrocity-prone-area instrument;
  • current V&MC membership, reconstitution and meeting compliance, including district/sub-divisional interface;
  • current implementation of the 6 March 2024 Special Court designation in day-to-day allocation;
  • current named SPP/ESPP roster, tenure and court allocation;
  • current relief-rate and payment workflow and case-level audit trail;
  • current victim/witness rights and referral workflow;
  • current contingency/prevention plan;
  • later Chandigarh-specific Section 21(4) reporting beyond the 2022 snapshot;
  • complete current Chandigarh Gazette/notification/circular corpus;
  • current post-BNS/BNSS/BSA police and prosecution transition instructions;
  • current court-wise prosecutor workload/performance records;
  • reconciliation of central-assistance data with case-level relief data.

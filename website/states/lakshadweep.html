Lakshadweep

Lakshadweep

Phase 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map Currentness reference date: 2026-09-06

1. Territorial and beneficiary scope

Lakshadweep is a single-district Union Territory. The current UT Administration describes ten inhabited islands and states that the indigenous population is classified as Scheduled Tribes; it also states that there are no Scheduled Castes in the Union Territory. This is a current administrative publication, not a substitute for the constitutional Scheduled Tribe instrument. [A: LAK-001]

The Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) (Union Territories) Order, 1951, as modified, lists throughout Lakshadweep the inhabitants of the Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands who, and both of whose parents, were born in those Islands, subject to the stated permanent-settlement proviso for children born on the mainland. [A: LAK-002]

The Constitution (Scheduled Castes) (Union Territories) Order, 1951 contains no Lakshadweep entry in the current consolidated text reviewed. The UT Administration expressly states that there are no Scheduled Castes in Lakshadweep. [A: LAK-003, LAK-001]

2. Local Scheduled-Tribe protection interface

Lakshadweep has a separate local protective-law interface. On 08 May 2026 the UT Administration published a public notice inviting comments on a draft amendment to the Laccadive, Minicoy and Aminidivi Islands (Protection Scheduled Tribes) Regulation. The notice closed on 07 June 2026. The draft is therefore evidence of an active 2026 local-law revision process, not evidence that the amendment was enacted. [A: LAK-004]

The 1974 adaptation of laws changed the name of the territory in the Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order and related laws to Lakshadweep. [A: LAK-005]

The local Protection Scheduled Tribes Regulation is treated as a related-law interface. Its current 2026 amendment status remains OPEN because the located material is a draft consultation notice and the underlying final instrument was not retrieved.

3. Central PoA framework

The Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 applies to Lakshadweep as a Central Act. Its institutional framework includes Special Courts/Exclusive Special Courts, Special Public Prosecutors/Exclusive Public Prosecutors, victim and witness rights, preventive machinery and implementation duties. [A: LAK-006]

Historical official Central implementation reporting records Lakshadweep as having a Special Court and Special Public Prosecutor arrangements, but those reports are historical and are not treated as a 2026 census. A 2015 official Central report recorded a PCR Cell under the Superintendent of Police, a Secretary-level Rule 9 Nodal Officer, no identified atrocity-prone area, a Rule 10 Special Officer arrangement, and a Special Court at Kavaratti. [A: LAK-007]

A separate official Central report recorded the District and Sessions Court, Kavaratti/Kozhikode arrangement as Special Court and Government Pleader/Assistant Public Prosecutor arrangements as Special Public Prosecutors. These historical descriptions are retained as provenance leads only. [A: LAK-008]

4. Current institutional architecture

The current UT Administration identifies Social Welfare & Tribal Affairs as the departmental nodal agency for welfare programmes for vulnerable and tribal communities. The current page does not identify a PoA-specific Protection Cell, Rule 9 incumbent, Rule 10 officer, V&MC or relief workflow. [A: LAK-009]

The current Police Department publication states that Police Headquarters is at Kavaratti and that police stations operate on the inhabited islands, with an Inspector at Minicoy and Sub-Inspector-level officers on the other island police stations. It describes general crime prevention and investigation functions, but does not establish a current PoA-specific Protection Cell, Rule 7 roster or Special Police Station matrix. [A: LAK-010]

The current judicial setup states that Lakshadweep is under the High Court of Kerala, with Munsiff/Judicial Magistrate courts at Andrott and Amini and a District and Sessions Court at Kavaratti. This establishes the general judicial architecture, not a current PoA section 14 notification. [A: LAK-011]

On 27 May 2026 the UT Administration established a Directorate of Prosecution under the Home Department headed by a Director of Prosecution under section 20 BNSS. This is a current prosecution-administration instrument. It does not itself establish a PoA-specific Special Public Prosecutor or Exclusive Public Prosecutor appointment. [A: LAK-012]

5. Current criminal-procedure transition

On 17 April 2026 the Lakshadweep Police/Home Department issued multiple notifications and rules under BNSS, including rules on admission/denial of documents, summary-trial forms and witness attendance. The witness-attendance rules contain current provisions for service, vulnerable-witness protections, reimbursement and electronic payment. [A: LAK-013]

These instruments establish active UT-level BNSS implementation, but they do not provide a complete PoA-specific transition matrix. The interaction between the Central PoA Act/Rules' legacy CrPC references and current BNSS operation remains part of the national transition workstream. [A: LAK-013, LAK-006]

6. Special Courts and prosecution

Historical official Central records establish that a Special Court existed for Lakshadweep and that Government Pleaders/Assistant Public Prosecutors were used as Special Public Prosecutors. [A: LAK-007, LAK-008]

Current official UT judicial and prosecution pages establish a Kavaratti District and Sessions Court and a new Directorate of Prosecution, but the targeted 2026 official-source search did not locate a current PoA-specific section 14 notification, Exclusive Special Court notification, SPP/ESPP appointment order or current PoA prosecutor roster. Search non-location is not treated as proof of legal absence. [A: LAK-011, LAK-012]

7. Rule 8, Rule 9, Rule 10 and V&MC machinery

The 2015 official Central implementation report is the latest located primary historical source used here for a PoA-specific Protection Cell, Rule 9 Nodal Officer, Rule 10 Special Officer and identified-area position. It reported a Protection Cell at Police Headquarters, a Secretary-level Nodal Officer, no atrocity-prone area and a Director Social Welfare and Tribal Affairs as Special Officer despite no identified area. [A: LAK-007]

No current 2026 primary instrument was located in the targeted UT Gazette, departmental, police, social-welfare and legal-repository search for a replacement/reconstitution of the Protection Cell, current Rule 9 named incumbent, current Rule 10 designation or current State/District/Sub-Divisional V&MC constitution. Search non-location is a controlled negative result, not proof of absence.

The located 2024 Lakshadweep annual-report submission is an official UT record dated February 2025 and expressly covers implementation of the PoA Act for the reporting year. The accessible copy was retrieved from a secondary host rather than the UT repository, so it is graded B and used as a reporting-period corroborative lead. Full field-level extraction remains OPEN. [B: LAK-014]

8. Investigation

The Central Rules require DSP-level investigation and provide the Rule 8 Protection Cell framework. [A: LAK-006]

Current Lakshadweep Police sources establish island police stations and general investigation responsibility, but the targeted search did not locate a current PoA-specific Rule 7 investigating-officer roster, 60-day compliance audit or Special Police Station designation. [A: LAK-010]

9. Relief, rehabilitation and victim/witness rights

The current Centrally Sponsored Scheme data record zero persons provided relief under the Scheme in Lakshadweep in 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25. This is scheme-specific reporting and is not proof that no relief was provided from UT resources. The same data record Central Assistance of 0.00 crore in 2020-21, 0.12 crore in 2021-22, 0.00 crore in 2022-23, 0.00 crore in 2023-24 and 0.00 crore in 2024-25. [A: LAK-015, LAK-016]

The current PoA Act contains statutory victim and witness rights under section 15A. [A: LAK-006]

The current District Legal Services Authority is demonstrably active in 2026. The UT Administration published an April 2026 notification concerning the superannuation of the DLSA Secretary and January 2026 DLSA activity. These sources establish current legal-services infrastructure, not PoA-specific legal-aid utilization. [A: LAK-017, LAK-018]

No current case-level relief register, payment workflow, expenditure reconciliation, rehabilitation audit, section 15A implementation scheme or PoA-specific legal-aid utilization dataset was located. These remain OPEN.

10. Section 21(4) reporting and annual reporting

The 2024 Lakshadweep annual-report submission demonstrates that the UT Administration has prepared and transmitted a PoA implementation report for a recent reporting year. The accessible copy is a secondary-host reproduction of an official UT document, so the report is retained as a Grade B corroborative source pending direct primary retrieval. [B: LAK-014]

The current 2026 search did not locate a directly retrievable 2025/2026 Lakshadweep Section 21(4) implementation report. Non-location does not establish non-submission.

11. Crime statistics

The latest located official MHA parliamentary response, using NCRB Crime in India data through 2023, records Lakshadweep as follows under crime/atrocities against STs: 0 cases registered in 2021, 0 in 2022 and 0 in 2023; in 2022 there was 1 case chargesheeted and 0 persons convicted, while the 2021 and 2023 charge-sheet and conviction fields were zero. [A: LAK-019]

For crimes/atrocities against SCs, the same official source records zero cases registered, chargesheeted, convicted, persons arrested, persons chargesheeted and persons convicted for Lakshadweep in 2021, 2022 and 2023. [A: LAK-019]

The 2019-2020 table records Lakshadweep with zero SC cases in both years and one ST case registered in 2020, with zero chargesheets/convictions/persons convicted. These are NCRB reporting figures, not proof of absence of unreported conduct or a complete implementation assessment. [A: LAK-019]

12. Current local administrative context

The current UT Administration describes Lakshadweep as a one-district UT with ten inhabited islands and states that its indigenous population is classified as Scheduled Tribes. The administration's current Police, Social Welfare & Tribal Affairs, judicial and prosecution pages show functioning territorial institutions in 2026. [A: LAK-001, LAK-009, LAK-010, LAK-011, LAK-012]

The current 2026 draft amendment to the local Protection Scheduled Tribes Regulation demonstrates that the related tribal-protection legal framework is under active review. The draft status must not be treated as enacted law. [A: LAK-004]

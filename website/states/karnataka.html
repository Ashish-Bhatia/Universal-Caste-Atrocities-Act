Karnataka

Karnataka State Implementation Inventory

Phase 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map Inventory date: 2026-09-05

Scope

Independent Karnataka inventory using current/recent official Karnataka and official Government of India sources. Historical reports are retained only for their reporting periods. Search silence is not treated as proof of absence. Final Central later-instrument completeness and BNS/BNSS/BSA transition verification remain separate.

1. State coordinating department and Rule 9 nodal architecture

The Government of India’s official Karnataka implementation reporting identifies the Principal Secretary, Social Welfare Department, Government of Karnataka as the Rule 9 nodal officer in its 2020 reporting. The 2022 official report likewise records the Principal Secretary, Social Welfare Department as nodal officer. A current 2026 primary appointment/designation order and present office-holder were not independently retrieved. Therefore the historical/official reporting establishes the reported architecture, not a frozen 2026 appointment.

2. Protection Cell / Directorate of Civil Rights Enforcement

Karnataka has a long-standing Civil Rights Enforcement Cell, later redesignated as the Directorate of Civil Rights Enforcement (DCRE). Official Government of India reports describe the DCRE as headed by an Additional Director General of Police, with regional offices and district units. The 2024 Karnataka State Annual Report states that, following the 1974 Government Order establishing a Civil Rights Enforcement Cell, Karnataka later established a full-fledged DCRE under an Additional Director General of Police, with Superintendents of Police heading divisions at Police Range headquarters.

Karnataka's 2025 State Budget and Assembly record state that 33 special police stations had been started under the DCRE. The 2026 record also contains evidence of continuing DCRE police-station operation. The underlying 2024/2025 establishment and staffing instruments should still be retrieved at instrument level before treating the 33-station structure as fully frozen.

3. Investigation architecture

The 2024 Karnataka State Annual Report describes the DCRE as a police establishment responsible, among other matters, for collection of intelligence and investigation of relevant cases and submission of reports to Government. The 2025 State Budget/Assembly record states that 33 special police stations had been started under DCRE. A 2026 secondary report states that the 33 stations operate as dedicated police stations for SC/ST atrocity investigations and identifies a 06-12-2024 government order as their establishment instrument, but the underlying State order was not retrieved in this inventory. Record the latter only as a source lead, not as an independently verified primary instrument.

4. Vigilance and Monitoring Committees

Official Government of India reporting records a State Level Vigilance and Monitoring Committee chaired by the Chief Minister, District Level Committees chaired by District Magistrates, and Sub-Divisional committees chaired by Sub-Divisional Magistrates. The 2022 report records 118 District V&MC meetings in 31 districts and 197 Sub-Divisional V&MC meetings in 53 sub-divisions. A January 2025 State-level V&MC meeting is independently corroborated by the National Human Rights Commission's official press-clipping record, which reports the Chief Minister's directions concerning investigation timeliness and quarterly district review meetings. A current 2026 constitution/membership matrix and complete meeting record set remain open.

5. Special and Exclusive Special Courts

The 2023 Karnataka State Annual Report records 31 districts, 23 district-level Special Courts and 8 Exclusive Special Courts. It identifies the eight Exclusive Special Court locations as Belagavi, Vijayapura, Kalaburagi, Kolar, Mysuru, Raichur, Ramanagar and Tumakuru, and states that District and Sessions Courts were designated as Special Courts in the other districts. The same report cites Notification No. HDj17/PCRj2001 dated 13-02-2001 as the basis described in the report. The 2024 State Annual Report continues to report Special Court and Exclusive Special Court arrangements, including specific Special Public Prosecutor/Exclusive Special Public Prosecutor fields.

These reports do not replace a current Gazette notification/jurisdiction matrix. Current High Court proceedings also demonstrate ongoing SC/ST Act Special Court operation in Bengaluru and other districts. A complete 2026 court-notification census remains open.

6. Special Public Prosecutors

The 2016 official Government of India Karnataka report records 22 Special Public Prosecutors for 22 designated Special Courts and 8 Exclusive Special Public Prosecutors for the eight Exclusive Special Courts. The 2024 Karnataka State Annual Report contains a dedicated district-level specification table for Special Public Prosecutors and Exclusive Special Public Prosecutors. A current appointment register and replacement orders were not retrieved. Therefore prosecutor architecture is established historically/currently at reporting level but not frozen by a complete 2026 appointment register.

7. Atrocity-prone areas

Official Government of India reports historically identify atrocity-prone districts/areas in Karnataka. The 2024 Karnataka State Annual Report records, for 2024, a 31-district table in which the retrieved entries show “No” for identified atrocity-prone areas and “No” for measures for the listed districts. This is a State annual-report response for 2024, not proof that no operative notification exists anywhere or at any other time. Primary notifications and any later amendments remain open.

8. Relief and rehabilitation

Current Government of India scheme data records Karnataka relief coverage of 2,483 persons in 2020-21, 2,582 in 2021-22, 3,090 in 2022-23, 3,740 in 2023-24 and 5,167 in 2024-25. Central assistance released was Rs.65.43 crore, Rs.61.85 crore, Rs.30.98 crore, Rs.40.00 crore and Rs.48.66 crore respectively. These figures establish official reported relief coverage and Central assistance, not case-level compliance, timing or State-only expenditure.

The 2020 official report records 1,078 Karnataka cases with investigation/charge-sheet filing within 60 days and 360 later than 60 days, and relief within seven days for 637 persons versus later relief for 1,023 persons. These are historical reporting-period figures and are not current 2026 performance data.

9. DCRE staffing / current administrative support

The 2026 secondary source reports a 27-07-2026 Social Welfare Department order, following Finance Department approval dated 21-07-2026, granting hardship allowance to 626 personnel serving in the 33 DCRE police stations. The reported allocation is 33 PSIs and 181 Head Constables at Rs.2,000/month each, and 412 Constables at Rs.3,000/month each. The underlying Government Order was not retrieved directly. Treat as a current source lead requiring primary instrument verification, not as final A-grade evidence.

10. PCR interface

The DCRE's historical mandate includes civil-rights enforcement alongside SC/ST atrocity work. Official Government of India reports identify the Civil Rights Enforcement Cell as Karnataka's state-level SC/ST protection structure. The current scope and operating relationship between PCR enforcement and the 33 DCRE police stations requires current State orders and staffing documents.

11. Manual Scavengers interface

The Government of India identifies Karnataka among States participating in manual-scavenger rehabilitation programmes. An official 2019 progress report records 2,474 persons identified in Karnataka under the 2013 and 2018 surveys, 648 recipients of one-time cash assistance from the 2013 survey cohort, and 190 beneficiaries for whom capital subsidy was released for self-employment projects. Current NAMASTE implementation is a separate Central programme and does not by itself establish Karnataka's current State enforcement/committee instruments. Current State-specific survey, enforcement and district/local-body instruments remain open.

12. Bonded Labour interface

The Karnataka Gram Swaraj and Panchayat Raj Act, 1993 contains section 58A, requiring a Gram Panchayat to report enforcement of the abolished bonded labour system to the Deputy Commissioner or another authority specified by Government. This is a directly relevant State statutory interface. Current Bonded Labour Vigilance Committee constitution orders and operational records remain to be retrieved from the Labour Department/State Gazette.

13. Panchayati Raj / local-government interface

The Karnataka Gram Swaraj and Panchayat Raj Act, 1993 is an operative State framework. Section 58B also imposes duties concerning practices that denigrate dignity, including duties relating to persons belonging to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, and reporting untouchability, social boycotts and other social exclusions to the concerned authority. These provisions are relevant to the State implementation interface but do not substitute for the PoA Rules' V&MC or Special Officer architecture.

14. Judicial currentness and post-BNS/BNSS practice

Current Karnataka High Court materials show SC/ST Act matters being litigated alongside BNS offences and under BNSS procedural provisions. For example, 2026 proceedings refer to BNS sections together with SC/ST Act provisions and use BNSS section 528 for inherent jurisdiction. This is evidence of post-01-07-2024 operational interaction. It is not a substitute for the separate project-wide BNS/BNSS/BSA transition verification.

15. Key qualifications

  • Current Rule 9 appointment/office-holder is not frozen by a 2026 primary designation instrument.
  • Current full DCRE establishment, staffing, police-station notification and district jurisdiction matrix is not frozen at instrument level.
  • Current V&MC constitution/membership and meeting matrix is not complete.
  • Current Special/Exclusive Special Court notification and jurisdiction matrix is not complete.
  • Current SPP/ESPP appointment register is not complete.
  • Current atrocity-prone-area notifications are not frozen.
  • Current relief-rate, sanction, treasury, DBT and case-level payment workflow is not frozen.
  • Current PoA-specific Gazette/SOP/circular/manual set is incomplete.
  • Current PoA-specific digital/FIR/case-management workflow is not independently established from primary State material.
  • Current PCR, Manual Scavengers and Bonded Labour State instruments remain partly open.
  • Current 2026 DCRE hardship-allowance order is a primary-source retrieval target because only a secondary report of the order was located.

Status

Karnataka is sufficiently inventoried for progression with qualifications. The strongest current evidence is the 2025 Karnataka Assembly/State Budget record of 33 DCRE special police stations, current/recent official Central data showing 5,167 persons provided relief in 2024-25 and Rs.48.66 crore Central assistance, official Karnataka State Annual Reports for 2023/2024 describing the Special Court/Exclusive Special Court and prosecutor architecture, the current/recent official Central IPS civil list showing DCRE leadership, and current Karnataka High Court records demonstrating continuing Special Court/SC-ST Act litigation. This inventory does not constitute a complete 2026 primary-instrument census.

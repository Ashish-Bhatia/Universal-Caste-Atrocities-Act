Phase 1 Priority Central Legislation Screening

Date: 2026-09-05

Scope

This is a screening pass, not a clause-by-clause crosswalk. It identifies whether each remaining Priority 2 Central instrument in legislation/SOURCE_MAP.md has a material interaction with caste-atrocity regulation, protected victim classes, remedies, evidence/procedure, administration, or consequential drafting.

The completed PCR, Manual Scavengers and Bonded Labour workstreams are not repeated here.

Instrument-specific verified findings

1. Forest Rights Act

India Code identifies the Forest Rights Act as Act 2 of 2007, enacted 29-12-2006 and enforced 31-12-2007. Its stated purpose is recognition and vesting of forest rights and occupation in forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers, with a statutory framework for recording rights and evidence. The Act contains provisions on forest rights, recognition/restoration/vesting, authorities and procedure, offences and penalties, and a non-derogation clause. citeturn0search7turn2search6

The Central Rules are identified on India Code as the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Rules, 2007. A Ministry of Tribal Affairs consolidated Rules publication records the 2012 amendment by G.S.R. 669(E), dated 06-09-2012. citeturn2search6turn2search36

Screening result: material. The Act must remain visible in the existing-law baseline because caste/tribe status, land and forest rights, Gram Sabha processes, evidence of rights and administrative decisions may intersect with alleged discriminatory dispossession or enforcement. No conclusion is made here about the adequacy or superiority of either regime.

2. Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act

India Code identifies the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 as Act 49 of 2016, enacted 27-12-2016. Its structure includes rights and entitlements, education, employment, social security, duties of governments, advisory bodies, commissioners, and a Special Court chapter. India Code's displayed metadata includes a stale-looking "Notification: yet to be notified" field while also identifying the Act as enacted legislation, so that metadata field is not used as an enforcement conclusion. citeturn0search14turn2search7

Screening result: material intersectional safeguard source. Relevant provisions should be treated as potentially cumulative where a victim is both caste-protected and disabled. Accessibility, reasonable accommodation, discrimination, abuse, institutional duties and procedural access require preservation if the eventual proposal affects investigation, courts or victim services.

3. Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act

India Code identifies the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 as Act 43 of 2005, enacted 13-09-2005 and enforced 26-10-2006. Its structure includes protection orders, residence orders, monetary relief, custody, compensation, interim/ex parte orders, jurisdiction, procedure, appeal, and a provision stating the Act is not in derogation of other law. The 2006 Rules are also listed. citeturn0search6turn2search4

Screening result: material remedies interface, not a substitute for caste-atrocity offences. Its non-derogation structure and civil/protective remedies are important when the same facts involve domestic violence and caste-based conduct.

4. POCSO Act

India Code identifies the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 as Act 32 of 2012, enforced 14-11-2012. The Act establishes offences involving sexual assault, sexual harassment and pornography and provides for Special Courts. citeturn0search8turn0search9

Screening result: high interaction where a child is a victim. A future caste-atrocity regime must avoid double-counting or silently displacing child-specific offences, Special Court arrangements, child-friendly procedure and evidence protections. Any concurrent-offence rule requires exact statutory drafting and later case-law review.

5. Juvenile Justice Act

India Code identifies the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 as Act 2 of 2016, enforced 15-01-2016. It covers children in conflict with law and children in need of care and protection, with dedicated chapters for Juvenile Justice Boards, Child Welfare Committees, procedures, rehabilitation/social reintegration and other offences against children. India Code lists the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Model Rules, 2016, notified by G.S.R. 898 on 21-09-2016. citeturn0search1turn0search2turn2search5turn2search34

Screening result: high child-protection interface. It is principally protective/rehabilitative rather than a caste-offence statute. Relevant provisions should remain cumulative where caste victimisation produces a child-protection or rehabilitation response.

6. Prevention of Corruption Act

India Code identifies the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 as Act 49 of 1988, enacted and enforced 09-09-1988. Its structure includes Special Judges, offences and penalties, investigation, attachment/forfeiture, and prosecution-sanction provisions. citeturn1search2turn1search4

Screening result: implementation-integrity interface. It becomes materially relevant if public officials manipulate registration, investigation, relief, rehabilitation, procurement or enforcement. It should not be treated as a caste-specific offence regime.

7. Representation of the People Act

India Code identifies the Representation of the People Act, 1951 as Act 43 of 1951, enacted and enforced 17-07-1951. Its long title covers elections, qualifications/disqualifications, corrupt practices and election offences, and election disputes. citeturn1search3turn1search5

Screening result: conditional electoral interface. Its relevance depends on whether the proposed legal architecture creates electoral consequences or addresses caste-related conduct in elections. No electoral offence or disqualification is inferred from this screening alone.

8. Child and Adolescent Labour Act

India Code identifies the Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986 as Act 61 of 1986, enacted 23-12-1986, with current displayed update date 25-03-2019. It prohibits child employment and adolescent employment in hazardous occupations/processes, and contains provisions on inspection, monitoring, rule-making, other laws and repeal/savings. India Code lists principal Rules dated 01-01-1988 and amendment Rules dated 02-06-2017. citeturn0search0turn0search5

Screening result: material labour interface. It overlaps factually with forced labour, exploitation and child victimisation. It must be preserved as a separate statutory regime because its ingredients, protected age categories, employer structure and regulatory machinery differ from caste-atrocity provisions.

Priority-screening conclusion

The current Priority 2 Central queue has been screened. The instruments fall into three operational groups:

  • Direct/intersectional substantive interfaces: Forest Rights, Child and Adolescent Labour, POCSO, Juvenile Justice, Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
  • Remedy/protection interfaces: Domestic Violence.
  • Administration/integrity/electoral interfaces: Prevention of Corruption and Representation of the People.

This screening does not close current-law completeness. India Code metadata and search results are discovery and navigation aids, not proof that no later Gazette instrument exists. Final Gazette/Legislative Department/Ministry completeness verification remains a Phase 1 freeze task.

Sequencing consequence

The remaining Priority 2 screening queue is now complete at screening level. The project may proceed to the State-by-State inventory after the authoritative-record remediation has been recorded and the control files updated. State inventory must still not begin before this screening workstream unless the project record is deliberately revised through the project's decision-control process.

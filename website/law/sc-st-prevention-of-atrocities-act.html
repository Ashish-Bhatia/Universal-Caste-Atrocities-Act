SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989, Clause-Level Extraction

Extraction date: 2026-09-05

Primary current-text source

India Code, Act No. 33 of 1989, PDF headed "As on the 21st September, 2025". The PDF identifies the amending Acts as the 2015 Amendment Act (1 of 2016), 2018 Amendment Act (27 of 2018), and Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 (34 of 2019). Evidence grade A. The India Code metadata page separately reports a last-updated indicator of 19-11-2018. The 2025 consolidated PDF demonstrates why the metadata date must not be treated as a completeness cutoff.

Section-by-section extraction

SectionCurrent operative subjectMaterial interaction / verification point
1Short title, whole-India extent, commencement by Central Government Gazette notification.Commencement instrument: S.O. 106(E), 29-01-1990, effective 30-01-1990.
2Definitions include atrocity, Code, dependent, economic boycott, Exclusive Special Court, forest rights, manual scavenger, public servant, SC/ST, Special Court, Special Public Prosecutor, Schedule, social boycott, victim and witness. Undefined expressions are tied to IPC, Indian Evidence Act and CrPC; references to enactments not in force are directed to corresponding law.High-priority BNS/BNSS/BSA interface. Legacy references require controlled transition verification.
3(1)Creates atrocity offences for a person who is not SC/ST. Conduct includes degrading treatment, land dispossession/interference, forced or bonded labour, carcass/grave work, manual scavenging, devadasi practices, electoral coercion, obstruction of elected SC/ST representatives, post-poll violence/boycott, retaliatory offences, false proceedings/information, public-view humiliation/caste abuse, sacred-object offences, enmity/hatred, disrespect to persons held in esteem, specified sexual conduct, contamination of common water, denial of customary/public access, forced displacement, obstruction of common/public resources and services, witchcraft-related harm, and social/economic boycott. General punishment is 6 months to 5 years and fine.Core conduct baseline for BNS crosswalk. Several clauses overlap factually with general and special criminal offences.
3(2)Aggravated offences for non-SC/ST offenders involving false evidence, fire/explosive mischief, specified serious IPC offences, Schedule offences, disappearance/suppression of evidence, false information, and offences by public servants.Schedule and several clauses remain textually expressed in IPC terms in the 2025 consolidation. BNS transition must be mapped, not assumed.
4Penalises wilful neglect by a non-SC/ST public servant of duties under the Act/Rules. Duties include reading back oral information, FIR registration and correct sectioning, furnishing copies, recording victim/witness statements, investigating and filing charge sheet within 60 days with written explanation for delay, correct preparation/translation of documents/electronic records, and other prescribed duties. Administrative inquiry recommendation is required for booking; Special/Exclusive Special Court takes cognizance and directs penal proceedings.Direct overlap with BNSS investigation/reporting duties and public-servant accountability.
5Enhanced punishment for subsequent conviction under Chapter II, minimum 1 year up to punishment for the offence.Special recidivist layer.
6Applies specified IPC provisions concerning common intention, general exceptions, abetment, conspiracy, attempt, section 149 and related provisions.Legacy IPC references require BNS correspondence verification.
7Special Court may forfeit property used for an offence after conviction and attach property during trial, with forfeiture on conviction to the extent required for fine.BNSS property-attachment and execution interface.
8Presumptions concerning financial assistance, group offences following existing disputes, and accused knowledge of victim/family.Direct BSA burden/presumption crosswalk required.
9State Government may confer specified police powers, including arrest, investigation and prosecution before Special Court, on State officers by Gazette notification; other officers must assist; Code applies so far as may be.BNSS police-power interface.
10Special Court may order externment of person likely to commit Chapter II offence in Scheduled/tribal or identified areas, up to three years, with grounds and representation/revocation mechanism.Preventive power. BNSS/public-order interface and constitutional review reserved for later phases.
11Enforces section 10 removal orders, including arrest/removal, temporary return by permission, conditions and revocation.BNSS arrest/custody interface.
12Court may require measurements/photographs of person subject to section 10; necessary means may be used; refusal is linked to IPC section 186; records are destroyed/returned after revocation.Legacy IPC reference requires BNS correspondence verification.
13Contravention of section 10 order punishable up to one year and fine.Substantive offence linked to preventive order.
14State Government, with Chief Justice concurrence, establishes Exclusive Special Courts for one or more districts and may specify Sessions Courts as Special Courts in lower-volume districts. Courts directly take cognizance. Two-month disposal/trial targets are stated as far as possible.BNSS court structure/direct-cognizance interface. State court notifications require inventory.
14AAppeals to High Court from non-interlocutory judgment/sentence/order on facts and law, including bail orders. 90-day period, outer 180-day limit, and three-month disposal target as far as possible.BNSS appellate/bail interface.
15State Government specifies/appoints Special Public Prosecutors and Exclusive Special Public Prosecutors; appointed advocates require at least seven years' practice.BNSS prosecutor/Directorate of Prosecution interface.
15AState protection duty for victims, dependents and witnesses; fairness, dignity and special needs; notice of proceedings including bail; participation and document/witness requests; hearing rights; protection, travel/maintenance, socio-economic rehabilitation and relocation; confidentiality and anti-harassment measures; complaint recording; free FIR copy; video recording; State scheme for relief/protection/information/medical precautions/legal aid/participation; assistance from NGOs, social workers or advocates.Major special victim/witness framework. Compare comprehensively with BNSS and related laws.
16Applies PCR Act section 10A, so far as may be, to collective fines under this Act.Direct PCR Act dependency.
17District Magistrate, SDM, other Executive Magistrate or police officer not below DSP may declare an area atrocity-prone and take preventive action after specified information/inquiry/reason to believe; specified CrPC chapters apply; State may issue preventive schemes.BNSS transition and preventive-procedure interface.
18Excludes CrPC section 438 anticipatory bail from cases involving accusation under the Act.BNSS section 482 is the current anticipatory-bail provision. The 16-07-2024 reference-construction notification is material.
18ANo preliminary inquiry for FIR registration; no approval required for necessary arrest; no procedure other than Act or Code applies; CrPC section 438 exclusion applies notwithstanding court judgment/order/direction. Inserted by 2018 Amendment Act, effective 20-08-2018.High-priority BNSS/arrest/bail interface. No case-law conclusion here.
19CrPC section 360 and Probation of Offenders Act, 1958 do not apply to persons over 18 convicted under Act.BNSS sentencing/probation interface requires mapping.
20Act overrides inconsistent law, custom, usage or instrument, unless otherwise provided.Special-law conflict rule. Requires BNS/BNSS/BSA/PCR conflict register.
21State Government must ensure effective implementation, including legal aid, travel/maintenance, rehabilitation, prosecution supervision, committees, surveys and identification of atrocity-prone areas. Central Government coordinates and annually lays report before Parliament.Administrative implementation baseline. Rules and State machinery are essential.
22Good-faith protection from suit, prosecution or other proceedings for specified acts under Act.General accountability/remedy interface reserved for later analysis.
23Central Government rule-making power; rules laid before both Houses for 30 days, subject to modification/annulment mechanism.Subordinate-law control.
ScheduleLists IPC offences for section 3(2)(va), including conspiracy, unlawful assembly/rioting, specified public-servant misconduct, hurt/grievous hurt, acid attack, hurt to deter public servant, wrongful restraint, specified sexual offences, kidnapping/abduction, criminal trespass, intimidation and insult to modesty.Critical BNS crosswalk dependency.

Amendment and current-version findings

  • The 2025 India Code consolidated PDF expressly lists the 2015 Amendment Act (1 of 2016), 2018 Amendment Act (27 of 2018), and Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 (34 of 2019).
  • Relevant 2015 Amendment Act provisions are shown as effective from 26-01-2016.
  • Section 18A was inserted by the 2018 Amendment Act and is shown as effective from 20-08-2018.
  • The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 removed the words excluding Jammu and Kashmir from section 1(2), effective 31-10-2019.
  • Targeted official-source searching located the India Code consolidation as on 21-09-2025 and did not locate a later Central amendment Act through 05-09-2026. This is not a completeness declaration. A final Gazette/Legislative Department index verification remains open.
  • Ministry of Law and Justice notification S.O. 2790(E), dated 16-07-2024, issued under section 8 of the General Clauses Act, provides that references in Acts, rules, notifications and similar instruments to IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act provisions are to be read as references to BNS, BNSS and BSA respectively, with corresponding provisions construed accordingly. This is material to the Act's legacy references in sections 2, 6, 12, 17, 18, 19 and the Schedule.

Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 and Protection of Civil Rights Rules, 1977: Section/Rule-Level Comparison

Date: 2026-09-05

Scope

This is a Phase 1 legal-baseline crosswalk between the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 (PCR Act), the Protection of Civil Rights Rules, 1977 (PCR Rules), and the already completed SC/ST Act/Rules baseline. It does not assess necessity, desirability, superiority, constitutionality, repeal policy or Bill design.

Primary-source basis: India Code PCR Act; India Code PCR Rules record; Government Gazette S.O. 3006 dated 15-09-1977; Department of Social Justice and Empowerment current publication index; Ministry of Law and Justice S.O. 2790(E), 16-07-2024; and existing project artifacts legislation/SCST_ACT_CLAUSE_EXTRACTION.md and legislation/SCST_RULES_CLAUSE_EXTRACTION.md. Evidence grade A applies to the underlying Government instruments. Consolidated/transcribed text is treated as a verification aid where the primary PDF is not machine-readable.

C. Exact correspondence by subject

  • Religious access: PCR s.3 maps most closely to SC/ST s.3(1)(za)(C), with fact-dependent overlap with (za)(A) and (y).
  • Public access, services and occupation: PCR ss.4-6 overlap extensively with SC/ST s.3(1)(za)(A)-(E), s.3(1)(y) and related clauses.
  • Boycott, retaliation and incitement: PCR s.7 overlaps with SC/ST s.3(1)(n), (o), (p), (r), (s) and (zc), but the statutory formulations differ.
  • Forced sanitation/labour: PCR s.7A overlaps with SC/ST s.3(1)(h)-(j) and, depending on facts, s.3(1)(zc).
  • Collective fines: SC/ST Act s.16 expressly applies PCR s.10A so far as may be. PCR Rules 3 and 4 therefore have direct relevance to the incorporated mechanism.
  • Presumptions: PCR s.12 and SC/ST s.8 are distinct special presumptions. Similarity is functional, not textual or element-for-element.
  • Public-servant accountability: PCR s.10 and SC/ST s.4 overlap in subject, but PCR uses deemed abetment while SC/ST s.4 creates a separate wilful-neglect offence.
  • Implementation: PCR s.15A and Rule 5 overlap administratively with SC/ST s.21 and the SC/ST Rules reporting machinery.

D. Transition basis

  • PCR s.10A(5) and s.15 retain CrPC-era references.
  • PCR Rule 3(4) refers to Chapter XXI of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973.
  • Ministry of Law and Justice S.O. 2790(E), dated 16-07-2024, is the relevant general transition instrument for legacy IPC/CrPC/Indian Evidence Act references in Acts, Rules and related instruments.
  • BNSS s.531 supplies the principal CrPC repeal/savings framework for proceedings pending immediately before 01-07-2024.
  • Direct numerical substitution is not assumed.
  • No separate PCR BNS offence crosswalk is created here because the identified PCR legacy references are primarily procedural rather than an IPC Schedule incorporated as substantive offence elements.

F. Verification limits

  • The PCR Act section structure and operative provisions were checked against the India Code Government record and consolidated text. Grade A.
  • PCR Rules 1-5 and S.O. 3006 were checked against Government/officially reproduced text. Grade A for the Gazette instrument.
  • The Department of Social Justice and Empowerment current index lists the PCR Act and PCR Rules as active publications. This is a current-source indicator, not proof that no later instrument exists.
  • Case law is not used to resolve meaning. That belongs to Phase 2.
  • No recommendation is made here to repeal, retain, merge or supersede any provision.
  • The Schedule item 43 / Annexure-II disability-notification discrepancy remains preserved and is unaffected by this workstream.

Workstream result

The PCR Act and PCR Rules comparison is complete at section/rule level for Phase 1, subject to the project's final Gazette/Legislative Department completeness pass. The most direct legal dependency is SC/ST Act s.16 incorporating PCR s.10A, which makes PCR Rules 3-4 relevant to collective fines under the SC/ST Act. The strongest substantive overlap is PCR ss.3-7A with multiple SC/ST s.3 offences. PCR s.12 and SC/ST s.8 are distinct special presumptions. PCR s.15A and Rule 5 overlap administratively with SC/ST s.21 and the SC/ST Rules. No policy or constitutional conclusion is drawn.

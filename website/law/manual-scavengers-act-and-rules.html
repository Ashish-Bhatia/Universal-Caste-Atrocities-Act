Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013 and Rules, 2013: Section/Rule-Level Crosswalk with SC/ST Act and Rules

Date: 2026-09-05

Scope and method

This artifact records a primary-source-led comparison of the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013 (MS Act) and the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Rules, 2013 (MS Rules) against the existing SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and SC/ST Rules, 1995, as previously extracted in the repository.

This is an existing-law crosswalk only. It does not recommend repeal, retention, merger or supersession and does not make policy, necessity or constitutional-validity conclusions.

Primary sources: India Code record for Act No. 25 of 2013; India Code Government PDF; National Commission for Safai Karamcharis official Act/Rules archive; Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment official material; Gazette instrument for G.S.R. 776(E), 12-12-2013. The machine-readable transcription of the Rules is used only as a verification aid where the official PDF is image-based. Evidence grade A applies to the underlying Government instruments; transcription aids are lower-grade support and do not override primary text.

Current-law and instrument status

  • India Code identifies Act No. 25 of 2013, enacted 18-09-2013 and enforced 06-12-2013. The India Code record identifies G.S.R. 776(E), dated 12-12-2013, as the Rules instrument. The Act record also lists an Act text incorporating the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 amendment to section 1(2). [A]
  • The National Commission for Safai Karamcharis official archive currently lists the 2013 Act, enforcement notification and 2013 Rules. Its page was last updated 04-09-2026. This is a current-source indicator, not proof that no later subordinate instrument exists. [A]
  • The official Department of Social Justice and Empowerment publication index lists the 2013 Act, enforcement notification and 2013 Rules. [A]
  • India Code currently lists subordinate notifications dated 13-01-2014, 28-02-2014, 07-08-2014, 16-02-2017, 02-05-2017 and 01-03-2018 under the Act record. These entries are not, by themselves, amendment Acts or Rules amendments. They require instrument-level classification. [A]
  • Official Gazette instruments identified in the current search include S.O. 3338(E), 26-07-2023, S.O. 4379(E), 06-10-2023 and S.O. 1510(E), 21-03-2024, all concerning composition/personnel of the Central Monitoring Committee under section 29. They are implementation notifications, not amendments to the substantive Act or the 2013 Rules. [A]
  • A 2022 private member's Amendment Bill was introduced in the Lok Sabha, but the retrieved material is a Bill, not an enacted amendment Act. It must not be treated as current law. [B/A for parliamentary document]
  • No later amendment Act to the MS Act or amendment Rules instrument was established by this workstream. Final Gazette/Legislative Department completeness verification remains open.

C. Annexures and SC/ST interaction

Annexure I

MS Rules Annexure I prescribes first-aid facilities and equipment requirements linked to Rule 7. It is an occupational-safety schedule, not an SC/ST victim-relief schedule. The SC/ST Rules Schedule serves a different function by prescribing relief contingencies. No equivalence should be inferred.

Annexure II

MS Rules Annexure II is the self-declaration/identification form for manual scavengers. It records personal, occupational, employment, social-background, benefit and alternative-occupation information and includes details of insanitary latrines. This is materially relevant to administrative records and potential evidence but does not create a special evidentiary presumption under the SC/ST Act.

Annexure III

MS Rules Annexure III is the inspector's notice form for hazardous sewer/septic-tank cleaning under Rule 13(8). It is an enforcement document within the MS Act framework. It has no direct SC/ST Rules equivalent.

E. High-priority overlap and divergence findings

  • MS Act ss.5-7 are the most important substantive overlap zone. Section 5 addresses insanitary latrines and employment/engagement as manual scavenger; section 7 addresses hazardous sewer/septic-tank cleaning. SC/ST Act s.3(1)(j) separately criminalises manual scavenging, while s.3(1)(h) addresses forced/bonded labour and other clauses may apply depending on facts. The regimes are therefore overlapping but not interchangeable.
  • The MS Act applies without requiring the offender to be non-SC/ST or the affected person to be SC/ST. The SC/ST Act's section 3 offence architecture generally requires a person who is not SC/ST and a specified protected victim/status condition. This is a core element distinction.
  • MS Act rehabilitation is status-based and occupational. SC/ST Act relief/rehabilitation is incident-based and tied to atrocity victims, dependants and witnesses. Similar terminology does not establish the same entitlement.
  • MS Act ss.20-23 create a sanitation-specific inspection and trial architecture. SC/ST Act ss.4, 9, 14 and 15 create a different public-servant, police, Special Court and prosecutor architecture.
  • MS Act s.22 makes every MS Act offence cognizable and non-bailable. This does not itself reproduce the SC/ST Act's section 18/18A anticipatory-bail exclusion or Special Court appeal regime.
  • MS Act s.23 creates dedicated company liability. No equivalent dedicated general corporate-liability provision appears in the extracted SC/ST Act structure.
  • MS Act ss.24-30 and Rules 10-14 create substantial monitoring, survey and reporting machinery that overlaps administratively with SC/ST Act s.21 and Rules 3-18. The two systems should remain separately attributed in any State implementation inventory.
  • MS Rules 3-8 are a detailed occupational-safety and emergency-response regime. They should not be treated as an evidentiary or procedural component of SC/ST prosecution merely because the same incident may involve both statutes.
  • MS Rule 13 expressly permits collection of photographs, video clips, samples, records and electronic-device material during inspection/enquiry, while the SC/ST framework has its own investigation/document/evidence interfaces. This creates a potential cross-statute evidence provenance issue for later case-law and BSA work.
  • MS Rules Annexure II and the resulting identification lists contain caste/religion and other personal information. This is an administrative record issue. It does not create a special SC/ST evidentiary presumption.

F. Transition and current-code interface

  • The MS Act and Rules contain CrPC-era procedural references, particularly in trial/procedure provisions and Rules. Current BNSS treatment requires the project's general transition matrix and must not be resolved by simple number substitution.
  • The project already treats S.O. 2790(E), dated 16-07-2024, and BNSS s.531 as the controlled general transition sources for specified legacy CrPC references.
  • This workstream does not create a new BNS/BNSS/BSA crosswalk because the immediate task is the MS Act/Rules to SC/ST Act/Rules comparison. Targeted current-code verification belongs in the final Phase 1 transition matrix.

G. Completeness limitations

  • The 2013 Central Rules are verified at instrument level through G.S.R. 776(E), dated 12-12-2013.
  • Current official NCSK and Department of Social Justice publication pages continue to list the Act and Rules, but archive/index presence is not a legal completeness certificate.
  • India Code lists multiple later notifications under the Act record. At least the 2023 and 2024 Central Monitoring Committee notifications identified in this workstream are implementation/personnel instruments rather than amendments to the substantive Act or Rules.
  • The 2022 Amendment Bill located in parliamentary/Gazette material is not an enacted law and is excluded from the current-law baseline.
  • No later amendment Act or amendment Rules instrument was established in this workstream. A final Gazette/Legislative Department completeness pass remains mandatory before Phase 1 freeze.
  • No case law is used to resolve disputed meaning. Case-law treatment belongs to Phase 2.
  • The Schedule item 43 / Annexure-II disability-notification discrepancy in the SC/ST Rules remains unchanged and is not affected by this workstream.

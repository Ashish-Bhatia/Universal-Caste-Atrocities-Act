Methodology

How the evidence is controlled.

The project separates source discovery, verification, interpretation and legislative design. Each stage has its own closure criteria.

Evidence grades

A

Primary authoritative

Constitution, legislation, Gazette, official judgment/order, Parliament or official Government record.

B

Strong official/credible

Authoritative institutional material with a limited provenance qualification.

C

Reliable secondary

Useful for leads and corroboration where primary retrieval is incomplete.

D

Weak/unverified

Never used as the sole basis for a major conclusion.

Currentness rules

Historical is not current

Annual reports and older implementation records are dated evidence. They are not silently converted into current State practice.

Search silence is not absence

If an instrument is not located, the record says unresolved or not located. It does not state that the instrument does not exist.

State-specific evidence

One State's administrative architecture is never used as a proxy for another State or Union Territory.

Provenance matters

A document's existence does not establish every proposition attributed to it. Operative legal claims require clause-level verification.

Project sequence

Phase 1
Existing-law baseline
Current statutes, Rules, implementation instruments and State/UT source map.
Phase 2
Case-law corpus
Material judicial decisions and interpretive analysis.
Phase 3
Statistics
Verified empirical evidence with definitions and denominators.
Phase 4
Constitutional review
Competence, rights, federalism, proportionality and judicial review.
Phase 5+
Comparative analysis, drafting, verification and publication
Only after prerequisite evidence is sufficiently established.

## Verified primary-source correspondence

1. BLSA Act section 2(g) explanation states that “abetment” has the meaning assigned to it in the Indian Penal Code, 1860.
2. BLSA Act section 20 provides the abetment offence and again uses the IPC-based definition.
3. BLSA Act section 21(1) states that an Executive Magistrate receiving State-conferred Judicial Magistrate powers is deemed, for purposes of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, to be a Judicial Magistrate of the first or second class, as applicable.
4. BLSA Act section 21(2) permits an offence under the Act to be tried summarily by a Magistrate.
5. BNS section 45 defines “Abetment of a thing”.
6. BNSS contains the current summary-trial regime in Chapter XXII, sections 283-289, and its section 3 is the general construction-of-references provision.
7. Legislative Department S.O. 2790(E), dated 16-07-2024, provides the controlling construction-of-references rule for legacy IPC and CrPC references in Acts and subordinate instruments, directing that such references be read respectively as references to BNS and BNSS and that corresponding provisions be construed accordingly.

## Controlled conclusions

The BLSA Act's IPC references to abetment are controlled transition references to the corresponding BNS provision, with BNS section 45 providing the current general abetment definition. The project does not assume that every consequence of the former IPC architecture is numerically identical to BNS.

The BLSA Act's CrPC reference in section 21(1) is a controlled transition reference to the corresponding BNSS framework. The summary-trial permission in section 21(2) is a special-law command. It is therefore not displaced merely because the BNSS contains its own summary-trial chapter. Current-law description should preserve the BLSA special provision and read its legacy CrPC reference through S.O. 2790(E) and the BNSS transition/savings framework.

# Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013 and Rules, 2013: Section/Rule-Level Crosswalk with SC/ST Act and Rules

Phase: 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map
Status: Completed with qualification for final current-law/Gazette completeness
Date: 2026-09-05

## Scope and method

This artifact records a primary-source-led comparison of the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013 (MS Act) and the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Rules, 2013 (MS Rules) against the existing SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and SC/ST Rules, 1995, as previously extracted in the repository.

This is an existing-law crosswalk only. It does not recommend repeal, retention, merger or supersession and does not make policy, necessity or constitutional-validity conclusions.

Primary sources: India Code record for Act No. 25 of 2013; India Code Government PDF; National Commission for Safai Karamcharis official Act/Rules archive; Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment official material; Gazette instrument for G.S.R. 776(E), 12-12-2013. The machine-readable transcription of the Rules is used only as a verification aid where the official PDF is image-based. Evidence grade A applies to the underlying Government instruments; transcription aids are lower-grade support and do not override primary text.

## C. Annexures and SC/ST interaction

### Annexure I

MS Rules Annexure I prescribes first-aid facilities and equipment requirements linked to Rule 7. It is an occupational-safety schedule, not an SC/ST victim-relief schedule. The SC/ST Rules Schedule serves a different function by prescribing relief contingencies. No equivalence should be inferred.

### Annexure II

MS Rules Annexure II is the self-declaration/identification form for manual scavengers. It records personal, occupational, employment, social-background, benefit and alternative-occupation information and includes details of insanitary latrines. This is materially relevant to administrative records and potential evidence but does not create a special evidentiary presumption under the SC/ST Act.

### Annexure III

MS Rules Annexure III is the inspector's notice form for hazardous sewer/septic-tank cleaning under Rule 13(8). It is an enforcement document within the MS Act framework. It has no direct SC/ST Rules equivalent.

## E. High-priority overlap and divergence findings

1. MS Act ss.5-7 are the most important substantive overlap zone. Section 5 addresses insanitary latrines and employment/engagement as manual scavenger; section 7 addresses hazardous sewer/septic-tank cleaning. SC/ST Act s.3(1)(j) separately criminalises manual scavenging, while s.3(1)(h) addresses forced/bonded labour and other clauses may apply depending on facts. The regimes are therefore overlapping but not interchangeable.
2. The MS Act applies without requiring the offender to be non-SC/ST or the affected person to be SC/ST. The SC/ST Act's section 3 offence architecture generally requires a person who is not SC/ST and a specified protected victim/status condition. This is a core element distinction.
3. MS Act rehabilitation is status-based and occupational. SC/ST Act relief/rehabilitation is incident-based and tied to atrocity victims, dependants and witnesses. Similar terminology does not establish the same entitlement.
4. MS Act ss.20-23 create a sanitation-specific inspection and trial architecture. SC/ST Act ss.4, 9, 14 and 15 create a different public-servant, police, Special Court and prosecutor architecture.
5. MS Act s.22 makes every MS Act offence cognizable and non-bailable. This does not itself reproduce the SC/ST Act's section 18/18A anticipatory-bail exclusion or Special Court appeal regime.
6. MS Act s.23 creates dedicated company liability. No equivalent dedicated general corporate-liability provision appears in the extracted SC/ST Act structure.
7. MS Act ss.24-30 and Rules 10-14 create substantial monitoring, survey and reporting machinery that overlaps administratively with SC/ST Act s.21 and Rules 3-18. The two systems should remain separately attributed in any State implementation inventory.
8. MS Rules 3-8 are a detailed occupational-safety and emergency-response regime. They should not be treated as an evidentiary or procedural component of SC/ST prosecution merely because the same incident may involve both statutes.
9. MS Rule 13 expressly permits collection of photographs, video clips, samples, records and electronic-device material during inspection/enquiry, while the SC/ST framework has its own investigation/document/evidence interfaces. This creates a potential cross-statute evidence provenance issue for later case-law and BSA work.
10. MS Rules Annexure II and the resulting identification lists contain caste/religion and other personal information. This is an administrative record issue. It does not create a special SC/ST evidentiary presumption.

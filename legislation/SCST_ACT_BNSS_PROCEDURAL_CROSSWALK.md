## 1. Scope and method

This artifact maps the procedural architecture of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and the identified procedural Rules interfaces against the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS).

Scope expressly includes: Act section 2 Code references; section 4 duties concerning FIR, statements, investigation and charge-sheet; section 7 property attachment/forfeiture; section 9 conferment of police powers; sections 10-13 externment and enforcement; section 14 Special/Exclusive Special Courts, direct cognizance and trial targets; section 15 prosecution; section 15A victim/witness procedural rights; section 17 preventive action; sections 18 and 18A anticipatory bail, arrest and preliminary-enquiry interfaces; section 19 probation/sentencing interface; section 20 override; Rule 7's DSP-level investigation and 60-day framework; and the BNSS repeal/savings framework.

Method: identify the Act/Rules procedural command, identify the closest BNSS provision or provisions, distinguish direct correspondence from special-law displacement, record residual differences, transition basis, duplication/conflict/gap/ambiguity and verification status. No policy, constitutional-validity or Bill-drafting conclusion is made.

## 2. Primary transition basis

BNSS Act 46 of 2023 was enacted on 25-12-2023 and enforced on 01-07-2024. BNSS section 4(2) provides that offences under other laws are dealt with under BNSS subject to any enactment regulating the manner or place of investigation, inquiry, trial or other treatment. BNSS section 5 preserves special/local laws and special jurisdiction, powers and forms of procedure unless BNSS specifically provides otherwise. These are the principal statutory interface provisions for the SC/ST Act's special procedures. India Code records the BNSS enforcement date as 01-07-2024. citeturn3search0turn3search1

BNSS section 531 repeals the CrPC and preserves pending appeals, applications, trials, inquiries and investigations under the CrPC where they were pending immediately before commencement. It also deems specified existing notifications, powers, forms, local jurisdictions, sentences, orders, rules and appointments under the CrPC to have been made under corresponding BNSS provisions, subject to the text of section 531. Therefore, the applicable procedural regime must be determined by the status of the particular proceeding at 01-07-2024, not by the date of the SC/ST Act or Rules provision itself. citeturn4search11turn4search6

The SC/ST Act's legacy CrPC references therefore require controlled transition analysis. Direct numerical substitution is not assumed. The Act remains a special law, while BNSS supplies general criminal procedure except where the Act prescribes a special procedure or jurisdiction. This follows expressly from BNSS sections 4(2) and 5. citeturn3search9

## 4. Detailed BNSS interfaces

### 4.1 Act section 4, investigation and charge-sheet

Section 4(2) of the Act imposes statutory duties on public servants. They include reading and recording oral information, registering the complaint/FIR under appropriate provisions, furnishing a copy, recording victim/witness statements, conducting investigation and filing the charge-sheet in the Special/Exclusive Special Court within 60 days with written explanation of delay, correctly preparing/framing/translating documents or electronic records, and performing other duties under the Act/Rules. Section 4(3) provides a special cognizance consequence for specified dereliction. The underlying Act text is verified from the current section text. citeturn5search18

BNSS correspondence:

- s.173, information in cognizable cases, is the principal FIR interface.
- s.175, police power to investigate cognizable cases, is the general investigation authority.
- s.176, procedure for investigation, governs investigation mechanics.
- ss.179-183 govern attendance/examination/statements/confessions.
- s.193 governs the police report on completion of investigation.

India Code's BNSS section index confirms these provisions. citeturn0search1turn3search0

Residual difference: the Act's 60-day duty is not replaced by the general BNSS investigation architecture. Rule 7(2) separately requires the DSP-level investigating officer to complete investigation on top priority and requires the police-station officer to file the charge-sheet in the Special/Exclusive Special Court within 60 days, inclusive of investigation and filing. Rule 7(2A) requires written explanation of delay. This remains a special-law deadline and must be analysed separately from BNSS custody/default-bail periods. The current Rules text identifies these requirements. citeturn7search25turn7search0

Verification status: correspondence verified; legal effect of breach of the 60-day rule remains outside this procedural mapping and is not treated as resolved.

### 4.2 Arrest

BNSS section 35 governs arrest without warrant. The current text includes conditions for arrest in cognizable offences, including the statutory decision framework for offences carrying imprisonment below or up to seven years. BNSS sections 36 onward contain arrest procedure and safeguards. The BNSS section index confirms sections 35-62 as the arrest chapter. citeturn0search31turn8search1

Act sections 9 and 18A interact with this framework. Section 9 allows State Government notification conferring Code police powers, including arrest, investigation and prosecution, on specified State officers, with other officers required to assist. Section 18A(1)(b) removes an approval requirement for arrest where necessary. citeturn5search10turn2search7

Residual difference: Act s.18A does not abolish BNSS arrest safeguards. It removes the special approval requirement. Arrest must therefore be tested against the current BNSS arrest provisions and the special Act text together.

### 4.3 Bail and anticipatory bail

Act section 18 excludes former CrPC section 438. Section 18A(2) repeats the exclusion notwithstanding judicial orders or directions. The current BNSS anticipatory-bail provision is section 482, while section 480 governs bail in non-bailable cases and section 483 gives special bail powers to the High Court/Court of Session. citeturn0search1turn2search7

Correspondence: CrPC s.438 -> BNSS s.482. The Act's exclusion therefore attaches to the current anticipatory-bail mechanism through controlled transition, not through retention of the repealed section number.

Act s.14A(2) separately creates an appeal to the High Court against an order granting or refusing bail by the Special/Exclusive Special Court. This is a special appeal route and must not be collapsed into the ordinary BNSS appeal provisions. citeturn2search6

Verification status: statutory correspondence verified. Judicial scope and exceptions remain deferred to Phase 2/4.

### 4.4 Special and Exclusive Special Courts, cognizance and trial

Act section 14 requires Exclusive Special Courts for one or more districts, or specification of a Court of Session as a Special Court where fewer cases are recorded. The specified/established courts have power to directly take cognizance. Section 14(2) requires adequate courts to ensure disposal within two months as far as possible, and section 14(3) requires day-to-day proceedings subject to recorded reasons for adjournment and a two-month trial target from filing of charge-sheet as far as possible. citeturn5search1turn5search9

BNSS general architecture includes Courts of Session and Judicial Magistrates in ss.6-23, with ss.210 and 213 addressing cognizance by Magistrates and Courts of Session. BNSS s.4(2) and s.5 expressly preserve special-law procedure and jurisdiction. citeturn3search0turn3search9

Exact correspondence: BNSS supplies the general criminal-court and trial procedure, while Act s.14 supplies the special forum, direct-cognizance authority and special speed requirements. There is no basis in BNSS alone to erase the Act's Special/Exclusive Special Court architecture.

### 4.5 Appeals

Act section 14A provides a special High Court appeal from judgments, sentences and non-interlocutory orders of the Special/Exclusive Special Court, both on fact and law, plus an appeal against orders granting or refusing bail. It prescribes a 90-day filing period, a sufficient-cause extension subject to its statutory provisos, and a three-month disposal target as far as possible. The current statutory text and judicial reproductions confirm this structure. citeturn2search6turn9search48

BNSS chapter XXXI contains the general appeal framework, including s.413, s.415 and ss.418-429. citeturn8search1

Exact correspondence: BNSS general appeal provisions are background procedure; Act s.14A is the specific appeal regime for covered SC/ST Special Court orders. The current judicial status of the 180-day proviso is not treated as settled in this Phase 1 crosswalk. A 2025 Himachal Pradesh High Court order reports the Supreme Court striking down of the second proviso to s.14A(3), and the matter should be incorporated into the later case-law corpus rather than silently modifying the statutory artifact now. citeturn2search17

Verification status: statutory interface verified; judicial effect of subsequent case law remains a Phase 2 item.

### 4.6 Victim and witness procedural rights

Section 15A creates special rights including protection; fairness, respect and dignity; notice of proceedings including bail; participation and hearing in specified proceedings; access to documents/material; protection and expenses; rehabilitation and relocation; identity protection; immediate response to intimidation complaints; recording of complaints; video recording of proceedings; and State schemes implementing specified entitlements. citeturn9search9

BNSS contains general victim/witness provisions including section 397, Treatment of victims, and section 398, Witness protection scheme. Sections 230-231 address supply of police-report and related documents in relevant cases. citeturn3search2

Exact interaction: BNSS supplies general victim/witness procedure, while section 15A remains a more specific SC/ST Act rights regime. No assumption is made that BNSS section 397/398 displaces the Act's more specific rights.

Verification status: correspondence verified; evidence-law interface remains open for BSA crosswalk.

### 4.7 Externment, arrest/removal and measurements

Act sections 10-13 form a distinct externment regime. Section 10 permits the Special Court, on complaint or police report and satisfaction of specified conditions, to direct removal from specified areas for a period not exceeding three years in the current text. It requires communication of grounds and permits representation within 30 days. Section 11 provides the enforcement procedure, including arrest/removal in specified non-compliance situations. Section 12 governs measurements and photographs and makes resistance/refusal a deemed offence under former IPC s.186. Section 13 creates a separate offence for contravening the Special Court order. citeturn4search5turn6search2turn6search10

BNSS chapter IX, ss.125-143, provides general security-for-peace/good-behaviour proceedings. BNSS chapter XII, ss.168-172, addresses preventive police action, and ss.35-62 address arrest. citeturn8search0turn8search1

Exact distinction: the SC/ST Act externment procedure is not the same legal mechanism as a BNSS security proceeding. The initiating court, protected statutory interest, caste-specific statutory trigger, area limitation and maximum period differ. BNSS provisions therefore operate only as supplementary general procedure where applicable. The Act's special procedure remains controlling where inconsistent.

Verification status: special-law distinction verified; former CrPC cross-reference in the Act's externment provisions requires controlled transition mapping in the final Phase 1 freeze.

### 4.8 Preventive action under Act section 17

Act section 17 permits specified Executive Magistrates and police officers of DSP rank or above, after information and such inquiry as considered necessary, to declare an area prone to atrocities and take preventive action. Section 17(2) applies former CrPC Chapters VIII, X and XI so far as may be. citeturn4search1turn4search4

BNSS replaces the CrPC and reorganises the relevant subject matter. Its chapter IX, ss.125-143, covers security for keeping peace/good behaviour; chapter XI, ss.148-167, covers maintenance of public order and tranquillity; chapter XII, ss.168-172, covers preventive police action. citeturn8search0

Exact correspondence is therefore subject-matter based rather than chapter-number based. The Act's section 17(1) power and “area prone to atrocities” mechanism remain special. The BNSS provisions supply the current procedural machinery corresponding to the former Code subjects, subject to Act ss.4(2), 5 and 20.

Verification status: correspondence class verified; detailed sub-section mapping of every former CrPC Chapter VIII/X/XI provision is a controlled extension item if required for final Phase 1 freeze.

### 4.9 Property attachment and forfeiture

Act section 7 permits the Special Court, after conviction, to forfeit property used for commission of the offence, and during trial to attach property belonging to the accused, with post-conviction forfeiture to the extent required to realise a fine. citeturn6search9turn6search14

BNSS chapter VIII contains a separate statutory regime concerning attachment and forfeiture, including ss.115-124, while BNSS ss.497-505 govern disposal and related property matters in the criminal-process context. citeturn8search0turn8search1

Residual difference: Act s.7 is a special-law property sanction tied to Chapter II offences and the Special Court. BNSS chapter VIII is a general regime with different triggers and institutional structure. These mechanisms must not be merged or treated as substitutes without provision-level analysis.

Verification status: correspondence class verified; no conclusion that one regime displaces the other.

### 4.10 Probation/sentencing interface

Act section 19 excludes former CrPC section 360 and the Probation of Offenders Act, 1958 for persons above eighteen years convicted of an offence under the Act. citeturn9search2

Because BNSS repeals the CrPC, former section 360 no longer exists as a current BNSS provision. The Act's special exclusion therefore requires transition-aware interpretation rather than simple renumbering. The Probation of Offenders Act remains a separate statute and is expressly named by section 19.

Verification status: current statutory relationship identified. Exact judicial treatment is deferred to case-law research.

### 4.11 Override and conflict

Act section 20 gives the Act overriding effect over inconsistent law, subject to the Act's own provisions. citeturn9search1 BNSS section 5 independently preserves special/local law, special jurisdiction and special procedure unless BNSS specifically provides otherwise. citeturn3search9

Operational rule for this crosswalk: where the SC/ST Act expressly regulates the matter, it is treated as the special procedure; where it relies on the Code without special modification, BNSS is the current general procedural source; where the old Code is expressly named, the transition provisions must be applied before identifying the current corresponding BNSS provision.

## 5. Rule 7 detailed interface

Rule 7(1): investigation by police officer not below DSP rank, appointed by State Government/DGP/SP with specified suitability considerations.

Rule 7(2): IO completes investigation on top priority, submits report to SP; SP forwards to DGP/Commissioner; officer-in-charge files charge-sheet in Special/Exclusive Special Court within 60 days, inclusive of investigation and filing.

Rule 7(2A): delay in investigation or charge-sheet must be explained in writing by IO.

Rule 7(3): senior State/Home/SC-ST development/prosecution/police authorities review investigation position quarterly.

These requirements are reproduced in the current consolidated Rules material and corroborated by a Government judicial-academy source. citeturn7search25turn7search0

BNSS interface:

- ss.175-176: investigation authority and procedure.
- ss.179-183: witness attendance, examination, statements and recording.
- s.187: procedure where investigation cannot be completed within 24 hours, including custody/default-bail framework.
- s.189: release where evidence is deficient.
- s.190: sending case to Magistrate where evidence is sufficient.
- s.193: police report on completion of investigation.

The 60-day Rule 7 framework is not a substitute for BNSS s.187 custody periods, nor is BNSS s.193 a repeal of Rule 7. The two operate at different normative levels and purposes. The Rule imposes a special investigation/charge-sheet duty and reporting chain; BNSS supplies the general procedural mechanics and judicial custody consequences.

Verification status: primary BNSS sections identified and special-law relationship verified. Exact consequences of breach remain open and must not be inferred from isolated recent judgments.

## 8. Commencement and transition rules

1. BNSS current general operation begins 01-07-2024. citeturn3search1
2. A proceeding that was an appeal, application, trial, inquiry or investigation pending immediately before 01-07-2024 remains governed by the CrPC under BNSS s.531(2)(a), subject to the complete text of the savings provision. citeturn4search11turn4search6
3. Certain pre-existing notifications, powers, forms, local jurisdictions, sentences, orders, rules and appointments are deemed to correspond to BNSS provisions under s.531(2)(b). citeturn4search6
4. A sanction or consent already accorded under CrPC for which proceedings had not commenced is treated under the corresponding BNSS provision under s.531(2)(c). citeturn4search6
5. The date and procedural status of each individual SC/ST Act matter therefore determine whether CrPC or BNSS procedure applies. The Act's special provisions continue to be tested against the applicable general code under BNSS ss.4(2) and 5.

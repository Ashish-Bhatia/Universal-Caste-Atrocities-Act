# Kerala, Phase 1 State Implementation Inventory

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: INVENTORIED WITH QUALIFICATIONS

## Scope and method

This inventory records Kerala-specific implementation evidence for the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and related interfaces. Current/recent official Kerala and official Government of India sources are preferred. Historical reports are retained only for their reporting periods. Search silence is not treated as proof of absence.

## 1. Administrative and nodal architecture

Current Kerala State Planning Board and Scheduled Tribes Development Department publications confirm continuing State-level implementation of the PCR/PoA framework. Historical official Central implementation reports identify the Principal Secretary, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes Development Department as the Rule 9 Nodal Officer. A current 2026 primary appointment/designation instrument has not yet been retrieved. Therefore the historical designation is recorded, but not frozen as the current office-holder arrangement.

## 2. Protection Cell and police machinery

Current State planning material states that one Scheduled Castes Protection Cell and Special Police Stations have been formed. Current Kerala Police material also provides a specific service channel for petitions regarding atrocities on SC/ST persons. The Right to Service page assigns a 10-day enquiry standard and identifies SMS Units and DySP/ACP in SDPOs as processing officers, with District Police Chief and Range DIG appellate roles. The Kerala Police Station House Management SOP requires a dedicated register for petitions against SC/ST and cites Circular No.19/2017 and Executive Directive 13/2019. Earlier State planning material records eight special mobile squads for PoA investigation, while the current STDD page specifically identifies a special mobile police squad in Wayanad. A complete current statewide unit/establishment matrix and underlying orders remain open.

## 3. Courts

Current State planning material states that Special Courts, including exclusive Special Courts, form part of the implementation architecture, and the STDD page states Special Benches have been constituted in all District Courts. Historical official Central reports identified four Special Courts at Kottarakkara, Mannarkkad, Mananthavady and Manjeri, with Sessions Courts designated in remaining districts. These historical details are not treated as a current 2026 notification census. The current number, jurisdiction and statutory notification basis of all Special/Exclusive Special Courts remain to be reconciled from current judicial instruments.

## 4. Prosecutors

Historical official Central reporting records Special Public Prosecutors specified for Special Courts. Current State publications confirm the continuing Special Court architecture but do not provide a complete current PoA-specific SPP/ESPP appointment register. Current appointments and replacement orders remain open.

## 5. V&MC and preventive geography

Historical Central implementation reports provide Rule 10 Special Officer and V&MC information for their reporting periods. Current State planning material refers to vigilance and monitoring activity but does not provide a complete current State/District/Sub-Divisional V&MC constitution matrix. Historical reports also differ on atrocity-prone-area identification. No current primary notification census has been established. No inference of present absence is made.

## 6. Relief, rehabilitation and legal assistance

The current Economic Review 2025 reports 154 atrocity victims receiving compensation grant in 2024-25 and describes one legal councillor in each district, one State legal advisor and three State-level call-centre assistants for atrocity complaints, compensation assistance, legal assistance and awareness. The current State Planning Board records a ₹1,500 lakh outlay for PCR/PoA implementation in the 2025-26 plan, while the STDD page records a ₹118 lakh State-share proposal for the ST PoA enforcement component for 2024-25, including ₹18 lakh for special-court expenditure. Current Central scheme data records 1,079, 1,177, 1,492 and 445 persons provided relief in 2020-21 through 2023-24, with 2024-25 marked NA. NA is not treated as zero. Current case-level sanction, treasury, DBT and payment workflow remains unverified.

## 7. Current police crime data

Kerala Police's statewide SC/ST Atrocities Act statistics report 976 cognizable cases in 2020, 1,081 in 2021, 1,222 in 2022, 1,313 in 2023, 1,269 in 2024, 1,059 in 2025 and 609 through July 2026. The figures are recorded as police crime-category data. They are not substituted for prosecution, conviction, acquittal, pendency or victim-relief measures.

## 8. PCR interface

Current State planning documents combine PCR Act and PoA Act implementation in the same administrative scheme. The current State planning architecture therefore establishes an operational interface, but a complete current PCR-specific order and reporting matrix has not been retrieved in this inventory.

## 9. Manual Scavengers interface

The current Kerala Local Self Government Department states that all 14 districts were declared manual-scavenging free by District Collectors in 2023. It records ₹54.80 lakh in one-time cash assistance to 137 workers and states that District and Sub-Divisional Vigilance Committees have been constituted in all districts. This is current State implementation evidence. It does not by itself establish complete instrument-level compliance or the full local-body enforcement record.

## 10. Bonded labour interface

The Kerala Law Department's official Acts repository publishes the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976. Historical official State material identifies District Magistrates as chairpersons of District Vigilance Committees under the Act. Current Labour Commissionerate material establishes district and sub-district labour enforcement machinery, but a current bonded-labour-specific committee order/register was not retrieved. No absence conclusion is drawn.

## 11. Local-government interface

The Kerala State Election Commission publishes the Kerala Panchayat Raj Act, 1994 and amendment materials. The Local Self Government Department also identifies the Panchayat Raj Act and Municipality Act as applicable instruments in sanitation and local-government administration. This provides the relevant State/local-body interface for implementation of related anti-discrimination, sanitation and rehabilitation measures. Detailed clause-level crosswalk is outside this state inventory and remains a Phase 1 closure task where needed.

## 12. Land and tribal-law interface

The Scheduled Tribes Development Department identifies implementation of the Kerala State Restriction in Transfer of Lands and Restoration of Alienated Land Act, 1999 as a current ST programme. The current department also identifies protection of tribal land rights as a core function. A full current instrument matrix for land-transfer/restoration and its interaction with PoA implementation is not frozen here.

## 13. Digital and post-BNSS transition

Current Kerala Police provides online/public petition and service mechanisms, including the specific 10-day SC/ST atrocity-petition enquiry service. The Station House Management SOP provides a dedicated SC/ST petition register. These sources establish operational channels, but a PoA-specific post-01-07-2024 DCRE/Police workflow and complete replacement of legacy CrPC terminology have not been established. General digital services are not treated as PoA-specific evidence without a direct instrument or workflow source.

## 14. Evidence limitations and residual issues

The principal current-source gaps are appointment and designation instruments, full police-unit establishment matrix, current court notification/jurisdiction matrix, current prosecutor register, current V&MC constitution, atrocity-prone-area notifications, relief-payment workflow, PoA-specific Gazette/SOP set, digital case workflow and post-BNSS instructions. Historical Central reports are used only for their reporting periods.

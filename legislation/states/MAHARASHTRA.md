# Maharashtra, State Implementation Inventory

Phase 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map
Inventory date: 2026-09-05
Status: INVENTORIED WITH QUALIFICATIONS

## Evidence discipline

Current Maharashtra sources are preferred. Historical Central reports are retained only for their reporting periods. Search silence is not treated as proof of absence. This is an implementation inventory, not a policy or constitutional conclusion.

## Verified implementation layer

The Maharashtra Home Department currently publishes the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989, Maharashtra Atrocities Rules, 1995 and Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955. Maharashtra Police maintains a dedicated Civil Rights Protection section with PoA procedure material, related Government Resolutions/circulars and a current monthly SC/ST/PCR statistics publication dated 21-08-2026. [MH-STATE-001 to MH-STATE-004, MH-STATE-017]

A primary Home Department decision dated 27-03-2026 extended the temporary ADGP, Civil Rights Protection post from 01-03-2026 through 31-08-2026. Current Police material also records 2026 extensions for Civil Rights Protection staffing and a recent revised establishment structure. Because the latest located extension ended 31-08-2026, status on 05-09-2026 is unresolved. [MH-STATE-005 to MH-STATE-007]

The 26-02-2019 State prevention circular requires sensitive-area identification, district and sub-divisional committee arrangements, vigilance/monitoring, special police deployment where needed, quarterly review, FIR/action, investigation, immediate assistance within seven days and training/capacity building. [MH-STATE-008, MH-STATE-019]

The current Social Justice & Special Assistance Department victim-relief scheme provides financial assistance to SC/ST atrocity victims. It links relief to the 23-12-2016 Government Resolution and describes police reporting to the Assistant Commissioner of Social Welfare followed by District Collector approval. [MH-STATE-009, MH-STATE-010]

The Social Welfare Commissionerate current objectives page states an amended SOP for atrocity cases and identifies State and District Vigilance and Monitoring Committees under the PoA Rules. The State Commission for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes states it reviews PoA/PCR cases and financial assistance to victims. [MH-STATE-011, MH-STATE-012]

## Reporting-period evidence

The 2019 official Central Maharashtra PoA report records a Secretary-level Rule 9 nodal officer, six identified atrocity-prone districts, each district Court of Session specified as a Special Court, three Exclusive Special Courts at Nagpur, Aurangabad and Mumbai (Thane), and 2,524 victims receiving relief. The 2022 report records a Principal Secretary-level Rule 9 nodal officer, no identified atrocity-prone area, the same three Exclusive Special Courts, and 1,408 investigations/charge-sheets within 60 days versus 1,453 later than 60 days. These are reporting-period records, not a 2026 census. [MH-STATE-013, MH-STATE-014]

Current Central scheme data records Maharashtra relief recipients of 2,245 in 2020-21, 3,339 in 2021-22, 2,869 in 2022-23, 3,339 in 2023-24 and 5,661 in 2024-25. Central assistance released was Rs.58.13 crore, Rs.7.73 crore, Rs.12.83 crore, Rs.37.28 crore and Rs.24.52 crore respectively. These are scheme-level figures, not case-level compliance measures. [MH-STATE-015]

Official Bombay High Court judgments identify Special Judges under the Atrocities Act in districts including Gangakhed/Parbhani, Aurangabad and Ahmednagar. They establish judicial operation at identified locations but do not establish a complete 2026 statewide court matrix. [MH-STATE-020]

## Status determination

Maharashtra is sufficiently inventoried for progression with qualifications. Current/recent official State sources establish a dedicated Civil Rights Protection function, recent establishment decisions, State publication of PoA legislation/rules, prevention guidelines, victim-relief architecture, current Police statistical publication and SC/ST institutional oversight. Official Central reports establish Rule 9, Special Court, Exclusive Special Court and sensitive-area arrangements for specified periods. The current 2026 instrument census remains incomplete. No historical reporting is treated as a 2026 census, and no absence is inferred from search silence.

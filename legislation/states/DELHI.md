# Delhi (NCT)

Phase 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map  
Status: PROCEED/CLOSE WITH LIMITATIONS  
Research date: 2026-09-05

## Scope

This jurisdiction inventory records Delhi-specific implementation material for the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and Rules, including beneficiary scope, administrative machinery, police investigation, courts/prosecutors, monitoring, relief and current/recent corroboration. It is an existing-law inventory. It does not assess policy superiority, necessity or constitutional validity.

## 1. Beneficiary scope and territorial context

Census of India 2011 provides district-wise and caste-wise Scheduled Caste datasets for the NCT of Delhi. [A: DL-STATE-001]

Official Census material states that no Scheduled Tribe has been notified in Delhi. The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes report likewise records that there is no Presidential notification of Scheduled Tribes under Article 342 for the NCT of Delhi. [A: DL-STATE-002, DL-STATE-003]

Accordingly, the Delhi PoA implementation baseline is principally an SC implementation baseline. The absence of a notified ST community in Delhi does not support any broader conclusion about statutory scope or the status of migrant ST persons.

## 2. Governing PoA instruments

The central SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 applies in Delhi. India Code records the Act, its amended structure and the 1995 Rules together with later amendments. [A: DL-STATE-004]

The Government of NCT of Delhi Department for Welfare of SC/ST/OBC publishes the PoA Act and Rules as operative instruments for Delhi and separately lists the PoA Act, PoA Rules and Protection of Civil Rights Act among its Acts/Rules. [A: DL-STATE-005]

The Delhi department reproduces the Rules, including Rule 8 Protection Cell, Rule 9 Nodal Officer, Rule 10 Special Officer, Rules 16-17 vigilance and monitoring committees and Rule 18 annual reporting. [A: DL-STATE-006]

## 3. Rule 8 Protection Cell and police machinery

The Delhi Police Citizen Charter states that an SC/ST Protection Cell is functioning in the Crime Branch of Delhi Police for handling complaints and matters relating to atrocities. It provides a dedicated contact number for the Cell. [A: DL-STATE-007]

The Delhi-specific administrative Rules page confirms the Rule 8 statutory functions, including survey of identified areas, public-order measures, review of investigations, review of registered cases and monthly reporting to the Nodal Officer. [A: DL-STATE-006]

A current 2026 named roster and internal staffing order for the Protection Cell were not independently retrieved. Search silence is not treated as proof of absence.

The 2020-21 Ministry annual report records Delhi as having an SC/ST Protection Cell. [B: DL-STATE-008]

## 4. Rule 9 Nodal Officer

The 2020-21 Ministry annual report records that a Rule 9 Nodal Officer had been appointed in the NCT of Delhi. [A/B: DL-STATE-008]

A historical Delhi PoA annual-report extract records the Secretary (Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes) as the Rule 9 Nodal Officer. [A: DL-STATE-009]

Current Delhi Department officer material, last updated in August 2026, identifies the senior SC/ST/OBC administrative structure, including the Additional Chief Secretary, Special Secretary and implementation branch. It does not independently state which named incumbent is formally appointed under Rule 9. [A: DL-STATE-010]

Therefore the existence of the Rule 9 office is established, while the current formal incumbent and controlling appointment instrument remain open.

## 5. Rule 10 / identified or atrocity-prone areas

The 2020-21 Ministry annual report records a Rule 10 Special Officer arrangement for Delhi. [A/B: DL-STATE-008]

The Ministry report's identified-area table does not list Delhi among the jurisdictions reported as having identified atrocity-prone/sensitive areas for that reporting period, while its Special Officer section separately records Delhi as having nominated Special Officers. [A: DL-STATE-011]

This distinction is material. Appointment of a Rule 10 Special Officer does not by itself establish that a current identified/atrocity-prone area exists.

The current 2026 Delhi Rule 3 identification record, current Rule 10 appointment instrument and present area determination were not independently retrieved. This remains open.

## 6. Vigilance and Monitoring Committees

The 2020-21 Ministry annual report records that State/UT-level and district-level vigilance and monitoring committees had been set up in the NCT of Delhi. [A/B: DL-STATE-008]

The Delhi Rules page reproduces Rule 17 requiring a district-level V&MC and quarterly meetings, and Rule 16's State-level committee framework. [A: DL-STATE-006]

The 2023-24 Ministry annual report records that the national coordination meeting on implementation of the PoA Act considered status of cases, chargesheets, court pendency, Exclusive Special Courts and Special Police Stations across States/UTs. [A: DL-STATE-012]

Current Delhi-specific committee membership, reconstitution instruments, meeting dates, minutes and compliance were not independently verified. This remains open.

## 7. Special Police Stations and investigation

The 2020-21 Ministry annual report identifies Delhi as having an SC/ST Protection Cell but the historical state-wise Special Police Station data did not place Delhi among the jurisdictions with dedicated Special Police Stations. [A/B: DL-STATE-008]

The Delhi Rules require PoA offences to be investigated by a police officer not below Deputy Superintendent of Police rank. [A: DL-STATE-006]

The current 2026 Delhi Police instrument establishing or confirming any dedicated Special Police Station for PoA offences was not independently retrieved. Search silence is not treated as proof of current absence.

## 8. Special Courts and Exclusive Special Courts

A historical Delhi PoA annual-report extract records an Additional District and Sessions Judge designated as Special Court in the NCT of Delhi. [A: DL-STATE-009]

The 2023 Parliamentary Standing Committee on Welfare of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes recorded Delhi's PoA case data and referred to 11 Special Courts available to fast-track such cases. [A: DL-STATE-013]

The Committee's Delhi data show 487 cases pending trial at the end of 2022. [A: DL-STATE-013]

The present 2026 court-wise Section 14/14A designation and whether any court is functioning as an Exclusive Special Court under the amended Act were not independently verified from a current Delhi judicial notification. The 11-court figure is therefore retained as a historical Parliamentary finding, not asserted as the current 2026 number.

## 9. Special Public Prosecutor / Exclusive Special Public Prosecutor

The historical Delhi PoA annual-report extract records an Additional Public Prosecutor/Special Public Prosecutor arrangement for PoA cases. [A: DL-STATE-009]

The Delhi Rules require specification of prosecution panels and periodic review of Special Public Prosecutor performance. [A: DL-STATE-006]

A current 2026 named SPP/ESPP roster, appointment notification, tenure and court allocation were not independently verified. This remains open.

## 10. Relief, legal aid and rehabilitation

The Delhi Welfare Department states that financial assistance is provided for rehabilitation of SC/ST persons who are victims of atrocities, subject to the PoA Act and Rules. Its current scheme page states that an FIR under section 3 of the PoA Act is required, the victim must belong to an SC/ST category and hold a caste certificate, and the victim must reside in or belong to Delhi. [A: DL-STATE-014]

The Department's current Annexure I page states that the District Magistrate, SDM or other Executive Magistrate is responsible for arranging immediate relief in cash or kind to victims, family members and dependants according to the Rules. [A: DL-STATE-015]

The 2020-21 Ministry annual report records central assistance to NCT Delhi of Rs. 48 lakh in 2018-19, Rs. 16 lakh in 2019-20 and Rs. 25 lakh in 2020-21 under the centrally sponsored PCR/PoA implementation scheme. [A: DL-STATE-008]

Current case-level relief payments, statutory rate application, payment timelines, victim/witness travel and maintenance, legal-aid referrals, rehabilitation records and reconciliation of central assistance with case-level payments remain open.

## 11. Statistical and institutional context

The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Welfare of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes recorded the following Delhi PoA data supplied by the Government of NCT of Delhi: 36 cases registered in 2018, 78 in 2019, 70 in 2020, 141 in 2021 and 130 in 2022. The same table records 487 cases pending trial at the end of 2022. It reports a 10% conviction rate for cases with trials completed in 2022, based on the Committee's table. [A: DL-STATE-013]

The Committee also recorded 33 cases registered up to 31 March 2023, 61 chargesheets up to 30 April 2023, 61 completed trials in the reported 2023 period and 487 cases pending trial at the end of that period. These figures are not full-year 2023 data and must not be treated as such. [A: DL-STATE-013]

The figures are historical administrative/statistical reporting and are not used here to infer causes, trends beyond the reporting definitions, or policy effectiveness.

## 12. Current/recent judicial and police corroboration

The Delhi High Court's 2025 proceedings include FIRs registered in Delhi under section 3(2)(v) of the PoA Act, including CRL.M.C. 3/2025 concerning FIR No. 904/2023, Police Station Wazirabad, and other 2025 proceedings involving PoA allegations. [A: DL-STATE-016, DL-STATE-017]

The Delhi High Court's 2026 judgment in BAIL APPLN. 1781/2025 and connected proceedings records FIR No. 529/2024, Police Station Dwarka North, under section 376 IPC and section 3(2)(v) of the PoA Act, with chargesheet and supplementary chargesheet filed. [A: DL-STATE-018]

These materials establish continuing judicial activity involving the PoA Act in Delhi. They are used only as current/recent institutional corroboration. No Phase 2 merits synthesis has been undertaken.

## 13. Related-law interfaces

The Delhi Welfare Department administers the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 alongside the PoA Act and publishes both instruments. [A: DL-STATE-005]

The NCT has a distinct Delhi Commission for Safai Karamcharis framework and other SC-focused administrative schemes. These are recorded as related-law interfaces where relevant, not as substitutes for PoA machinery. [A: DL-STATE-005, DL-STATE-014]

No jurisdiction-specific tribal protective regime comparable to the special territorial regimes identified in some other UTs was located for Delhi. The constitutional/statutory absence of a notified Delhi ST community is separately recorded and is not treated as evidence against claims of migrant or non-local tribal persons.

BNS/BNSS/BSA transition verification remains a separate national workstream. The current Delhi High Court cases above are not used to infer complete police/prosecution transition status.

## Disposition

PROCEED/CLOSE WITH LIMITATIONS. Delhi has a documented PoA administrative framework, including a Rule 8 Protection Cell, Rule 9 Nodal Officer, Rule 10 Special Officer arrangement, V&MC machinery, Special Court arrangements and SPP machinery. Current Delhi government pages continue to publish and administer the PoA Act/Rules and relief framework. The Delhi Police Citizen Charter independently confirms a functioning SC/ST Protection Cell in the Crime Branch. [A: DL-STATE-005, DL-STATE-006, DL-STATE-007, DL-STATE-014]

The principal evidence gaps concern current appointment/designation instruments, current court and prosecutor rosters, current committee compliance, current identified-area status, case-level relief auditability and later Section 21(4) reporting.

No Bill drafting, constitutional-validity analysis, policy-superiority/necessity analysis or Phase 2 case-law corpus work was undertaken.

# Nagaland

## Phase 1 Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map

Status: PROCEED/CLOSE WITH LIMITATIONS
Research date: 2026-09-05
Evidence rule: primary-source preference. Historical reporting is separated from current status. Search silence is not treated as proof of absence.

## 1. Jurisdictional baseline

Nagaland has no Scheduled Caste community specified in the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order. The Department of Social Justice and Empowerment's Annual Report 2021-22 records that no community has been specified as Scheduled Caste for Nagaland. The same report records 0.00% SC population for Nagaland in Census 2011. Nagaland does have Scheduled Tribes, and the Constitution (Nagaland) Scheduled Tribes Order, 1970 covers the State's notified ST communities.

This makes the PoA Act's ST limb the principal jurisdictional interface identified in the present inventory. It does not justify treating Nagaland as outside the Act merely because there are no notified SCs.

## 2. Article 371A interface

Article 371A(1)(a) provides that, notwithstanding the Constitution, no Act of Parliament concerning specified matters, including Naga customary law and procedure and administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to Naga customary law, applies to Nagaland unless the Nagaland Legislative Assembly resolves otherwise. Ownership and transfer of land and its resources are also protected subjects.

The PoA Act is a central criminal statute and its application therefore requires careful separation of ordinary criminal-law enforcement from matters governed by Naga customary law. The present inventory does not conclude that Article 371A exempts the PoA Act. Actual Nagaland Police and court records demonstrate use of the PoA Act in Nagaland-related proceedings, including a 2023 Dimapur FIR and a 2025 Nagaland High Court proceeding involving charge-sheeting under the Act.

## 3. Rule 8 Protection Cell

Historical Union reporting did not establish a current dedicated Nagaland SC/ST Protection Cell. A 2023 parliamentary/official compilation records Nagaland among predominantly tribal States where special implementation machinery was not comparable to the general State model. Current dedicated Protection Cell status remains unverified.

## 4. Rule 9 Nodal Officer

A current Nagaland Rule 9 Nodal Officer was not located in the present pass. No inference of non-appointment is made.

## 5. Special Police Stations / investigation machinery

No current dedicated Special Police Station under the PoA Act was verified. Historical Union material treated Nagaland as an exceptional predominantly tribal jurisdiction with limited special machinery. Current Nagaland Police materials located in this pass do not identify a dedicated PoA Special Police Station.

The 2023 Dimapur FIR demonstrates that Nagaland Police register and investigate PoA Act offences, but does not establish the institutional structure used for every such case.

## 6. Rule 10 / atrocity-prone areas

Historical Union reports repeatedly described Nagaland as having no reported atrocities or no need for special measures. The 2022 reporting dataset available through official Ministry material records zero cases for Nagaland in the relevant historical tables, while a later official NCRB-derived Ministry publication notes that clarifications from Nagaland were pending for the 2022 dataset.

No current Rule 10 Special Officer or current identified atrocity-prone area was verified. These are open currentness questions, not findings of absence.

## 7. Vigilance and Monitoring Committees

Historical Union reporting records State/District monitoring arrangements in varying forms across States, but the present research pass did not locate a current Nagaland-specific Rule 16/17/17A constitution and meeting record. This remains unresolved.

## 8. Special Courts

Historical parliamentary material records that Nagaland, like Arunachal Pradesh and Mizoram, was among predominantly tribal States that had not notified existing Sessions Courts as Special Courts under the general arrangement at the time of the report. The present pass did not locate a later Gazette establishing a Nagaland PoA Special Court or Exclusive Special Court.

This is not a current negative finding. The 2024 Delhi High Court proceedings concerning a Nagaland FIR expressly contemplated the accused approaching the competent court in Nagaland, and a 2025 Gauhati High Court proceeding records a Nagaland Police charge-sheet under the PoA Act. The present pass does not establish the exact current trial-court designation.

## 9. Special Public Prosecutors

A current Nagaland Special Public Prosecutor or Exclusive Special Public Prosecutor appointment specific to the PoA Act was not verified. Historical material does not provide a reliable current appointment record.

## 10. Relief, legal aid and victim/witness safeguards

No current Nagaland-specific Rule 11/12/15/15A implementation order, payment workflow, legal-aid protocol or contingency plan was verified in this pass. This is a documentation/currentness gap, not evidence that relief or legal aid is unavailable.

## 11. Annual reporting and statistical position

The Ministry's Annual Report 2021-22 states that Nagaland had no notified SC population and reproduces NCRB-based 2020 data showing 0 cases registered under the PoA Act for Nagaland. A separate Ministry page records publication of the annual report under Section 21(4) for calendar year 2022 on 17-09-2024. The official 2022 report was identified, but the Nagaland-specific implementation text was not reliably retrievable during this pass. A secondary 2026 analysis reproduces Nagaland as having no reported charge-sheet or legal-aid figures in the 2022 table, but this is not used as the sole basis for a major conclusion.

The 2023 Dimapur FIR and subsequent proceedings demonstrate that a zero or NIL historical statistical return must not be interpreted as meaning the PoA Act is legally irrelevant or incapable of being invoked in Nagaland.

## 12. Current/recent judicial and police corroboration

A 2024 Delhi High Court proceeding arose from FIR No. 69/2023 registered at Police Station Dimapur under IPC provisions and sections 3(1)(r), 3(1)(s) and 3(1)(u) of the PoA Act. The Court's judgment records the State of Nagaland's position and dealt with transit-bail jurisdiction.

A 2025 Gauhati High Court proceeding involving the State of Nagaland records that a charge-sheet was filed under sections 3(1)(w)(i) and 3(1)(w)(ii) of the PoA Act along with BNS provisions, with the victims belonging to a Scheduled Tribe community. This is strong current evidence that the PoA Act remains operational in Nagaland-related criminal proceedings.

## 13. Related-law interfaces

The principal jurisdiction-specific interfaces requiring preservation for later phases are:

- Constitution Article 371A, especially customary law/procedure and criminal justice involving decisions according to customary law.
- Constitution (Nagaland) Scheduled Tribes Order, 1970 and the State's ST/IIC certification architecture.
- Nagaland Rules of Administration of Justice and Police, 1937, as preserved in the State's legal framework and discussed by the Gauhati High Court.
- Village Council Act, 1978 and village-level customary/local administration.
- BNS/BNSS/BSA transition, which remains a separate national Phase 1 workstream and is not frozen here.

## 14. Phase 1 disposition

PROCEED/CLOSE WITH LIMITATIONS.

The inventory establishes a strong jurisdictional baseline: Nagaland has no notified SC population, the PoA Act remains capable of being invoked for ST victims, Article 371A creates a material constitutional/local-law interface, and recent proceedings demonstrate operational use of the Act. Current institutional implementation remains incompletely documented. The principal unresolved questions concern current Rule 8/9 machinery, Special Police Station status, Rule 10/atrocity-prone areas, V&MCs, Special/Exclusive Special Courts, SPP/ESPP appointments, relief/legal-aid workflows, current annual reporting and reconciliation of official statistics with recent cases.

No policy or constitutional-superiority conclusion is drawn from the Nagaland inventory.

# Tamil Nadu

Phase 1, Existing-Law Baseline and Source Map
Status: PROCEED/CLOSE WITH LIMITATIONS
Research date: 2026-09-05

## Scope

This jurisdiction inventory covers Tamil Nadu-specific implementation material for the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 and Rules, related Protection of Civil Rights interfaces, State institutional arrangements, police machinery, courts/prosecutors, relief, monitoring committees and recent official corroboration. It is an existing-law inventory, not a policy-superiority or constitutional-validity conclusion.

## 1. Governing instruments and State publication

Tamil Nadu's Adi Dravidar and Tribal Welfare Department is the State's principal administrative department for SC/ST welfare. Its current public-facing portal identifies the Department and its Commissionerate/Directorate structure. [A: TN-STATE-001]

The Tamil Nadu Government Gazette records publication of the 2016 SC/ST PoA Amendment Rules in the State Gazette and continuing Home Department notifications concerning Special Public Prosecutors and Special Courts under the PoA Act. [A: TN-STATE-002, TN-STATE-003]

The Tamil Nadu 2022 Section 21(4) report records a State Model Contingency Plan under Rule 15 notified in the Tamil Nadu Government Gazette on 01-09-2017 and communicated to police commissioners and district superintendents. [A: TN-STATE-004]

## 2. Rule 9 nodal architecture

A 2024 Tamil Nadu implementation/status report states that G.O.(Ms.) No.18, Adi Dravidar and Tribal Welfare (PA) Department, dated 20-01-2003, appointed the Secretary to Government, Adi Dravidar and Tribal Welfare Department as the Rule 9 Nodal Officer for coordination of District Magistrates, Superintendents of Police and other implementing officers. [A/B: TN-STATE-005]

The located source establishes the cited appointment order, but a current 2026 instrument or successor appointment was not independently retrieved. Current named-office verification therefore remains open.

## 3. Rule 8 Protection Cell and police machinery

The 2022 Section 21(4) report states that the ADGP/IGP and AIGP, Social Justice & Human Rights, monitor enforcement of the PoA Act and supervise Social Justice and Human Rights Units. It records 38 such Units at district headquarters, supported by mobile squads for prevention/detection and statistical units. [A: TN-STATE-004]

A current 2026 Madras High Court order identifies an Additional Director General of Police, Social Justice and Human Rights, as a functioning State police office in Tamil Nadu. This corroborates continuing institutional existence but does not itself establish the complete current Rule 8 staffing matrix. [A: TN-STATE-006]

A 2024 Tamil Nadu implementation/status report states that all police stations in the State are empowered to register PoA cases and that there are no dedicated Special Police Stations for PoA offences. This is the strongest located recent State-specific statement on the point. [A/B: TN-STATE-005]

## 4. Rule 7 investigation and charge-sheet timing

The 2022 Section 21(4) implementation data records Tamil Nadu with 690 charge-sheets filed within 60 days and 617 after 60 days. These are calendar-year 2022 reporting figures and are not treated as a 2026 compliance rate. [A/B: TN-STATE-007]

The 2022 report also records 1695 persons receiving travelling and maintenance expenses and 3563 atrocity victims receiving relief. [A: TN-STATE-004]

Current post-BNS/BNSS/BSA police/prosecution transition instructions and a current case-level Rule 7 compliance series remain open.

## 5. Rule 10 / identified and atrocity-prone areas

The 2022 Section 21(4) Tamil Nadu report states that all District Collectors had been appointed Special Officers under G.O.(Ms.) No.96, Adi Dravidar and Tribal Welfare (PA) Department, dated 10-08-2006, and that Government Letter No.9685/PA2/2015-9 dated 06-03-2017 instructed District Collectors to perform Rule 10 duties and submit reports. [A/B: TN-STATE-008]

The same report states that, during 2022, Social Justice and Human Rights Units conducted awareness/social-justice activity in atrocity-prone villages and recorded 988 village visits. [A: TN-STATE-008]

The Tamil Nadu Statistical Handbook records 430 atrocity-prone villages and 414 dormant atrocity-prone villages in 2022, and 394 atrocity-prone plus 479 dormant villages in 2023. It attributes the series to the ADGP, Social Justice and Human Rights, Chennai. [A: TN-STATE-009]

A 2022 PIB answer separately reported 345 villages in 37 districts and 27 villages in seven Commissionerates as identified atrocity-prone for the 2020 reporting context. The differing counts are retained as separate reporting snapshots and are not merged. [A: TN-STATE-010]

## 6. Vigilance and Monitoring Committees

The 2022 Section 21(4) report records one State Level Vigilance and Monitoring Committee meeting under the Chief Minister during 2022, 130 District Level meetings across 38 districts, and 133 Sub-Divisional meetings. [A: TN-STATE-004]

The 2022 report states that District Committees review PoA implementation, relief and rehabilitation and prosecution issues, and that Sub-Divisional Committees were constituted in 38 districts with instructions for regular meetings. [A: TN-STATE-004]

A 2023 Madras High Court proceeding concerning Sub-Divisional Vigilance and Monitoring Committees records litigation seeking their constitution and regular meetings. It is retained only as implementation-process corroboration, not as Phase 2 merits research. [A: TN-STATE-011]

## 7. Special and Exclusive Special Courts

The 2022 Section 21(4) report records 37 Special Courts and 14 districts with Exclusive Special Courts for Tamil Nadu, with the remaining 15 districts using designated Sessions Courts as Special Courts. [A: TN-STATE-004]

A 2026 Tamil Nadu Gazette entry records constitution of a Special Court in the cadre of District Judge for PoA cases at Tenkasi District, demonstrating continuing State-level court designation activity. [A: TN-STATE-003]

A 2025 Tamil Nadu Gazette entry also records an appointment of a Special Public Prosecutor for PoA cases in the Principal District and Sessions Court, Ranipet, while a June 2025 Gazette entry records another PoA Special Public Prosecutor appointment for Tirupathur. [A: TN-STATE-012, TN-STATE-013]

The current district-by-district 2026 Special/Exclusive Special Court census remains incomplete. A 2026 secondary analysis reproduces 17 Tamil Nadu districts with Exclusive Special Courts, sourced to the Ministry's 2022 report, but this secondary count is retained only as corroboration and does not replace direct State/Ministry verification. [C: TN-STATE-014]

## 8. SPP/ESPP

The 2022 Section 21(4) report states that 32 Special Public Prosecutors were specified for designated Special and Exclusive Special Courts in Tamil Nadu. [A: TN-STATE-004]

The Tamil Nadu Gazette shows continuing district-specific PoA Special Public Prosecutor appointments in 2025. [A: TN-STATE-012, TN-STATE-013]

The current 2026 named SPP/ESPP roster, tenure and allocation across all courts remain unverified. The 32-person 2022 figure is therefore historical implementation evidence only.

## 9. Relief, rehabilitation and victim/witness expenses

The 2022 Section 21(4) report states that 3563 atrocity victims belonging to SC/ST communities received relief under Rule 12(4), and 1695 persons received travelling and maintenance expenses. It also states that requests for free legal aid are brought under the Tamil Nadu Legal Services Authority scheme. [A: TN-STATE-004]

A 2024 Tamil Nadu status report records State-level relief and rehabilitation circular activity, including a 2022 ADGP circular and 2023 rehabilitation direction, and notes enhanced relief provisions in 2021. [B: TN-STATE-015]

The current case-level payment workflow, seven-day statutory compliance, current rates after later State enhancements, victim/witness referral workflow and 2026 payment statistics remain open.

## 10. State PoA/PCR instruments and related-law interfaces

The 2016 PoA Rules amendment was published in the Tamil Nadu Gazette. [A: TN-STATE-002]

The State's 2017 Model Contingency Plan was gazetted and communicated to implementing police authorities. [A: TN-STATE-004]

The current Adi Dravidar and Tribal Welfare Department portal continues to operate an SC/ST-focused administrative and service layer. [A: TN-STATE-001]

Tamil Nadu also operates a distinct Social Justice and Human Rights police structure for PoA/PCR enforcement. The 2022 report describes the Social Justice and Human Rights Units as the operational enforcement structure and records separate mobile and statistical functions. [A: TN-STATE-004]

## 11. Annual reporting and statistics

The Ministry's 2022 Section 21(4) report is the principal recent implementation snapshot located. It records: one State V&MC meeting; 130 District V&MC meetings; 133 Sub-Divisional meetings; 38 Social Justice and Human Rights Units; 37 Special Courts; 14 Exclusive Special Court districts; 32 SPPs; 3563 victims receiving relief; 1695 persons receiving travel/maintenance expenses; and 988 awareness programmes. [A: TN-STATE-004]

The same report records 690 charge-sheets within 60 days and 617 after 60 days. [A: TN-STATE-007]

The Ministry's 2022 crime table records 1684 SC and 61 ST PoA cases in Tamil Nadu in 2022, total 1745. [A: TN-STATE-016]

Tamil Nadu Police's official 2019 crime compendium records 1060 SC atrocity cases in which the PoA Act applied, with district-level variation, and separately identifies 84 cases where only the PoA Act applied. This is retained as a historical police-statistics series, separate from the Ministry's implementation report. [A: TN-STATE-017]

## 12. Legal-aid and victim-support interface

The 2022 Section 21(4) report states that requests for free legal aid are handled through the Tamil Nadu Legal Services Authority. [A: TN-STATE-004]

The 2024 Tamil Nadu status page identifies continuing legal/implementation circulars and monthly/status reporting infrastructure, including 2025 status material. It does not itself establish the completeness of a current PoA-specific victim-support case-management workflow. [B: TN-STATE-015]

## 13. Current/recent judicial and police corroboration

A 2026 Madras High Court order identifies the ADGP, Social Justice and Human Rights as a respondent in a case involving allegations of atrocity and directs the District Superintendent of Police to conduct an enquiry. This confirms continuing institutional operation of the Social Justice and Human Rights police office in 2026. [A: TN-STATE-006]

A 2026 Madras High Court order in a PoA prosecution identifies an Exclusive Special Court for SC/ST PoA cases at Sivagangai and records a charge-sheet pending before that court. The order is retained only as jurisdictional and institutional corroboration, not as a Phase 2 merits synthesis. [A: TN-STATE-018]

## Disposition

PROCEED/CLOSE WITH LIMITATIONS. Tamil Nadu has a comparatively detailed documented implementation structure: a Rule 9 appointment order is identified, Social Justice and Human Rights police units operate across the districts, dedicated Special Police Stations are reported absent, Rule 10 Special Officer architecture is documented, State/District/Sub-Divisional V&MCs are documented with 2022 meeting data, Special and Exclusive Special Courts are documented, SPP appointments continue to appear in recent Gazettes, and relief/legal-aid mechanisms are documented. Current 2026 instrument and roster verification remains incomplete.

No Bill drafting, constitutional-validity analysis, policy-superiority/necessity analysis or Phase 2 case-law corpus work was undertaken.

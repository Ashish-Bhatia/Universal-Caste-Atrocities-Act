### 1.1 Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016

India Code currently lists the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Rules, 2017, a 21-07-2017 corrigendum, the 08-03-2019 Amendment Rules and the 17-03-2020 Amendment Rules. The official India Code Gazette PDF for the 2020 amendment identifies G.S.R. 181(E), dated 17-03-2020, and records the amendment to Rule 31. The Department's current India Code record also lists later current administrative notifications/guidelines through 2022.

Disposition: later-instrument class advanced. The identified Central Rules sequence is 2017, corrigendum 2017, amendment 2019 and amendment 2020. No later amendment is asserted absent a broader Gazette census.

### 1.2 Representation of the People Act, 1951

The India Code Gazette PDF for 17-06-2022 confirms the Conduct of Elections (Second Amendment) Rules, 2022 and states that the principal Rules were last amended by S.O. 72(E) before that instrument.

## 2. SC/ST Act BNS correspondence advances

### 2.1 Controlling transition instrument

Legislative Department S.O. 2790(E), dated 16-07-2024, issued under section 8 of the General Clauses Act, provides that references to the IPC, CrPC or Indian Evidence Act in Acts, State Acts, Ordinances, article 240 regulations, Presidential orders and rules/regulations/orders/notifications made under them are to be read respectively as references to BNS, BNSS or BSA and corresponding provisions are to be construed accordingly. This is a construction-of-references rule, not blanket numerical substitution.

### 2.2 BNS Schedule correspondence requiring material-difference controls

The primary BNS text confirms material changes relevant to the SC/ST Act Schedule and section 3(2)(va) threshold analysis. BNS section 116 reduces the eighth grievous-hurt duration threshold from 20 days under IPC section 320 to 15 days. BNS section 117(2) retains a maximum seven-year punishment for voluntarily causing grievous hurt, while BNS section 115(2) provides up to one year or fine up to Rs.10,000 or both for simple hurt. BNS section 146 creates a general unlawful compulsory labour offence punishable up to one year, which is relevant to, but not equivalent with, SC/ST Act section 3(1)(h). BNS section 358 repeals the IPC while preserving prior operation, accrued rights/liabilities, penalties and proceedings, and treats actions taken under the IPC as taken under corresponding provisions of the Sanhita.

Disposition: these provisions strengthen the existing crosswalk's material-difference controls. They do not close the full section 3(2)(v) and Schedule correspondence because the SC/ST Act imports underlying punishment thresholds and some BNS provisions reorganise or merge IPC offences.

### 2.3 Rule 2(ga), "voluntarily"

The existing project finding remains controlled: Rule 2(ga)'s IPC section 39 reference corresponds to BNS section 2(33) under S.O. 2790(E). No change to the prior status.

### 2.4 BNSS procedural correspondence

The current BNSS India Code record identifies sections 3, 4 and 5 as the preliminary construction/special-law provisions, sections 173-193 as the current information/investigation/reporting sequence, sections 283-289 as Chapter XXII summary-trial provisions, and section 531 as repeal and savings. This confirms the structural correspondence already advanced for former CrPC references. The existing Rule 7 SC/ST Rules framework and its 60-day investigation consequence still require exact subject-matter verification against the enacted BNSS text and the special-law command before closure.

The PCR Rule 3(4) summary-trial correspondence to BNSS Chapter XXII and the BLSA transition record remain controlled in their separate artifacts. No arithmetic substitution is adopted.

### 2.5 BSA evidence/presumption correspondence

The current BSA India Code record identifies sections 104-109 as the general burden-of-proof sequence and section 115 as a general statutory-presumption provision. This confirms the existing project's distinction between the SC/ST Act's special section 8 presumptions and the BSA's general burden/presumption architecture. No claim is made that BSA section 115 reproduces SC/ST Act section 8. Judicial operation remains outside this Phase 1 closure pass.

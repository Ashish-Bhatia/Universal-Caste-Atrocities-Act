## Scope control

This extension addresses the next authorized Central later-instrument dependency after Child and Adolescent Labour: the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 and Central Rules/instrument class. It also corrects the prior Extension 05 Forest Rights endpoint because new primary evidence establishes a post-2018 Central amending Act. It does not reopen State/UT inventories, PCA Extensions 06-08, RPA Extension 09, Child and Adolescent Labour Extension 10, section 3(2)(v), the BNS/BNSS/BSA freeze, RPwD/JJ residuals, website verification, drafting, policy analysis, constitutional analysis or Phase 2 case-law research.

## Baseline

India Code identifies the Forest Rights Act as Act 2 of 2007, enacted 29-12-2006 and brought into force on 31-12-2007 by S.O. 2224(E). The current India Code Act text states in section 1(2) that the Act extends to the whole of India, with the former Jammu and Kashmir exclusion removed by Act 34 of 2019, section 95 and the Fifth Schedule, with effect from 31-10-2019.

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs current knowledge hub continues to publish the Forest Rights Act 2006 together with the Rules, including the 2012 Amendment and Guidelines. The Government-hosted consolidated Rules booklet identifies G.S.R. 669(E), dated 06-09-2012, as the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Amendment Rules, 2012.

## Newly verified post-2018 Central amending Act

### Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, Act 34 of 2019

The primary India Code text of the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 records Act No. 34 of 2019, assented to on 09-08-2019. Section 95 and the Fifth Schedule amend the Forest Rights Act by omitting the words “except the State of Jammu and Kashmir” from section 1(2). The amendment is effective from 31-10-2019.

Classification: Central amending Act affecting the territorial extent of the Forest Rights Act.
Evidence grade: A.

This is a material correction to Extension 05. The prior statement that no post-2018 Central amendment Act to the Forest Rights Act had been located is superseded by this primary evidence.

The amendment does not itself establish every operational consequence in Jammu and Kashmir or Ladakh. Operational implementation evidence must remain separately controlled in the State/UT inventories and later administrative instruments.

## Newly verified post-2018 Central administrative/statutory instrument

### S.O. 2631(E), 07-06-2022

The Ministry of Home Affairs Gazette notification S.O. 2631(E), dated 07-06-2022, issued under article 239(1) of the Constitution, directs that the Administrator (Lieutenant Governor) of the Union territory of Andaman and Nicobar Islands shall, subject to Presidential control and until further orders, also exercise the powers and discharge the functions of the State Government under the Forest Rights Act within that Union territory.

Classification: Central constitutional/administrative instrument affecting exercise of State Government functions under the Act in a Union territory. It is not an amendment to the Act or Central Rules.
Evidence grade: A.

The Ministry of Home Affairs current notifications register independently lists S.O. 2631(E) with the same subject and date.

## Rules endpoint and later-rule search

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs current knowledge hub lists the Forest Rights Act with Rules including the 2012 Amendment. The Government-hosted consolidated Rules booklet records G.S.R. 669(E), 06-09-2012, as the identified Central amendment Rules instrument and contains footnotes to the operative insertions/substitutions made by that instrument.

Targeted official-source searches did not retrieve a subsequent Central amendment to the Forest Rights Rules after G.S.R. 669(E). This is retrieval silence, not legal absence. The project therefore retains the 2012 Rules text as the controlled Rules endpoint while separately recording the later 2019 Central Act amendment and 2022 Central administrative instrument.

## Current territorial significance

The post-2018 Central Act amendment materially changes the historical territorial baseline. The Forest Rights Act is no longer controlled as a statute excluding Jammu and Kashmir. Official Ministry material also records implementation in the Union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh after the 2019 reorganisation, but such implementation evidence is not used to replace the primary Act amendment.

The 2025 Government of Jammu and Kashmir order designating the Tribal Affairs Department as nodal department and the Administrative Secretary as nodal officer is retained as corroborative current implementation evidence, not as a Central amendment or Central rule-making instrument.

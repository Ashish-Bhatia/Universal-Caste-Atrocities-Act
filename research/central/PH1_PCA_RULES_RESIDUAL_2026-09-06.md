## Scope

This record addresses only the unresolved Central Prevention of Corruption Rules class. It does not treat India Code silence as proof that no rules exist and does not reopen completed Central classes.

## Primary statutory control

The current India Code text of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 records section 29A, inserted by the Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, 2018 with effect from 26-07-2018. Section 29A(1) authorises the Central Government, by notification in the Official Gazette, to make rules for carrying out the Act. Section 29A(2) expressly identifies, among other matters, guidelines for commercial organisations under section 9, guidelines for sanction of prosecution under section 19(1), and other matters required or permitted to be prescribed. Section 29A(3) requires rules made under the section to be laid before both Houses of Parliament for the statutory period.

Primary source: India Code, Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, current text, section 29A.

## Targeted primary-source search

### Department of Personnel and Training

Targeted DoPT searches were conducted for:

- Prevention of Corruption Rules
- Prevention of Corruption Act Rules
- section 29A rules
- commercial organisation section 9 rules/guidelines
- section 19 sanction guidelines
- section 17A SOP/guidance

The official DoPT material located identifies administrative guidance and prosecution-sanction procedures rather than an instrument expressly issued as rules under section 29A. The DoPT Vigilance Handbook states that the latest guidelines on prosecution sanction were issued by DoPT O.M. No. 372/19/2012-AVD-III dated 03-05-2012. DoPT annual reports also describe processing of requests for sanction under the Prevention of Corruption Act and refer to the 2012 OM.

This is evidence of administrative guidance and practice. It is not evidence that section 29A rules do not exist.

### Legislative Department

Targeted official Legislative Department searches for Prevention of Corruption Act rule-making and section 29A did not identify a Central instrument titled as Prevention of Corruption Rules or Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Rules under section 29A in the retrieved official results.

This is a negative search result only. It is not legal-absence evidence.

### eGazette

Targeted official eGazette searches were run for combinations of Prevention of Corruption, Prevention of Corruption Rules, Prevention of Corruption Amendment Rules, section 29A and related rule terminology. No qualifying Central rule instrument was retrieved from the searched results.

Again, this is an indexed/retrieval result, not proof of non-existence. The eGazette search endpoint and indexing are not treated as exhaustive unless an instrument is independently located and verified.

## Important classification distinction

Do not classify every Central instrument concerning corruption as a rule under the Prevention of Corruption Act. The following are separate source classes unless their legal authority expressly establishes otherwise:

1. DoPT office memoranda and administrative guidelines.
2. CVC guidance and circulars.
3. Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act rules, including rules that define or reference offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act.
4. Central Civil Services, All India Services or other service rules concerning disciplinary/vigilance administration.
5. Statutory rules made under another parent Act.
6. State vigilance or anti-corruption rules.
7. Rules made under section 29A of the Prevention of Corruption Act itself.

Only class 7 closes the PCA Rules residual.

## Controlled conclusion

The Prevention of Corruption Rules class remains OPEN under PH1-ISSUE-008.

The strongest verified position is:

- Parliament has expressly conferred Central rule-making power under section 29A.
- DoPT maintains administrative guidance and procedures concerning prosecution sanction under the Act.
- The targeted DoPT, Legislative Department and eGazette searches did not retrieve a qualifying section 29A Central Rules instrument.
- The project must not convert this retrieval silence into a legal-absence finding.
- A later closure attempt should seek either an actual section 29A instrument and its Gazette/Parliament-laying trail, or stronger primary evidence establishing the authoritative Central repository and a complete negative census.

Date: 2026-09-06

## Scope

This extension examines the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 later-instrument dependency beyond the completed Extensions 06-08 section 29A Rules census. It is limited to: post-2018 Central amendment Acts; post-2018 Central operative notifications/orders affecting the PCA statutory text or machinery; Central statutory instruments under PCA powers other than section 29A Rules where primary evidence establishes the power and legal effect; and relevant parliamentary legislative history.

Extensions 06-08 are not repeated. No State/UT research, Bill drafting, policy analysis, constitutional analysis, or Phase 2 case-law research is included.

## Verified findings

### 1. No later Central amendment Act to the PCA was identified after Act 34 of 2019 in the defined search

India Code's current PCA record lists the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 and the Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, 2018 as the amending Acts in its principal Act PDF. The current consolidated text expressly records the 2019 Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act as altering section 1(2). No separate Central Act enacted after Act 34 of 2019 was retrieved in the targeted official-source searches as amending the PCA itself. This is a qualified retrieval result, not a legal-absence conclusion.

### 2. Act 34 of 2019 materially changed the PCA's territorial application

The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, Fifth Schedule item 75, read with section 95, removes “except the State of Jammu and Kashmir” from section 1(2) of the PCA, with effect from 31-10-2019. The current India Code PCA text carries the same amendment footnote. This is an enacted Central amendment affecting the PCA after the 2018 amendment Act and must be retained in the Phase 1 current-law map.

### 3. The 2020 Adaptation of Central Laws Order made further PCA machinery changes for J&K

S.O. 1123(E), dated 18-03-2020, was issued by the Ministry of Home Affairs under section 96 of the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019. Its PCA entry inserts section 17B, establishing the J&K Anti-Corruption Bureau, and sections 17C-17G, creating a J&K-specific seizure/attachment, designated-authority, appeal and forfeiture framework. The Order expressly states immediate effect and contains general savings/continuity provisions for prior acts and instruments.

This is a Central statutory adaptation order with direct operative effect on PCA machinery in J&K. It is distinct from the completed section 29A Rules census and must not be classified as a rule under section 29A.

### 4. A 2025 Central Gazette notification affects PCA prosecution machinery, but its metadata requires re-verification

The official eGazette search result dated 31-05-2025 records an amendment to the Government of India notification S.O. 8 dated 28-12-2015 concerning appointment of a Special Public Prosecutor for CBI PCA cases in Madhya Pradesh. The retrieved text states that serial no. 4 and the related entry were omitted. This demonstrates post-2018 Central operative activity concerning PCA prosecution machinery.

The underlying official eGazette PDF could not be reopened in this environment because the PDF endpoint returned HTTP 502. Therefore the exact notification number, full title and enabling statutory provision are retained as a verification residual. The legal effect described above is limited to what the official search result expressly states.

### 5. Section 3 appointment notifications are ordinarily Central or State notifications, but the present search did not establish a complete Central census

PCA section 3 authorises the Central Government or State Government, by notification in the Official Gazette, to appoint Special Judges for specified areas/cases. Current official State records show numerous post-2018 section 3 notifications, but State instruments are outside this Central later-instrument workstream. The targeted Central search did not establish a sufficiently complete nationwide Central section 3 notification register. No legal absence is inferred.

### 6. Section 29A Rules remain excluded from this extension

Extensions 06-08 already completed the section 29A Rules search at a qualified negative-source endpoint. No part of that census is repeated here. The present extension therefore does not claim that no section 29A Rules exist.

## Parliamentary legislative history

The 2018 amendment Act was considered by Parliament in 2018. The Lok Sabha debate of 24-07-2018 records the Minister moving consideration of the Bill as passed by Rajya Sabha and describes the Bill as a substantial revision of corruption offences, including active bribery. The Parliamentary Digital Library records the Bill as passed in Lok Sabha on 24-07-2018 and the Rajya Sabha message recording passage on 19-07-2018.

The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Bill, 2019 was considered and passed by Parliament in August 2019. The Parliamentary Digital Library records the 06-08-2019 combined debate and adoption of the Bill. The precise parliamentary discussion of the PCA Schedule item was not separately isolated in the retrieved material. Accordingly, no claim is made here that Parliament specifically debated the PCA amendment beyond the Bill's enactment and Schedule adoption.

The 2020 Adaptation Order was executive action under section 96 of the 2019 Act, not a parliamentary amendment Act. Its legal basis and operative text are therefore recorded from the Gazette instrument itself.

## Negative-source qualification

Targeted official searches did not retrieve a separate post-2019 Central amendment Act expressly amending the PCA after Act 34 of 2019. They also did not establish a complete Central census of every post-2018 section 3 Special Judge notification or every possible administrative order touching PCA prosecution/sanction machinery. India Code indexes and Ministry pages are treated as discovery/current-source indicators, not exhaustive Gazette registers.

Accordingly, Extension 14 is ADVANCED WITH QUALIFICATION. It does not establish legal absence.
